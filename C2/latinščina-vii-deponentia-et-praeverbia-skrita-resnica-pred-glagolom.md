## *Veritas occulta — The Hidden Truth Before the Verb*

**METODUS MOJE:** **EMOTIO + CONFLICTUS + FABULA + ETYMOLOGIA**

**PRAEVERBIA SEMPER** — every lesson from now on carries its verbs **cut by preverbs**. The preverb is not decoration — it is the **direction of the action**, the hand that turns the verb.

**Neolatin:**

## 🔥 **I. EMOTIO — ČUSTVO** (The Emotion of Hidden Truth)

### **THE TWO FACES OF THIS LESSON**

**1. DEPONENTIA — the verb that wears a mask**

- **Passive in form** — *loquitur* (he is spoken)
- **Active in meaning** — "he speaks"
- **Like:** Luka the **lutka/agent** — the puppet-form carried by the acting being
- **Like:** the master who serves — passive appearance, active essence

**2. PRAEVERBIA — the direction before the verb**

- **dūco** (I lead) → **ē-dūco** (I lead OUT → I educate)
- **dūco** (I lead) → **in-dūco** (I lead IN → I introduce)
- **dūco** (I lead) → **rē-dūco** (I lead BACK → I reduce, I return)
- **One root, many directions. The preverb chooses the path.**

**Neolatin:**
> *Deponens est larva cum corde. Praeverbium est manus quae verbum vertit.*
> *Forma passiva, animus activus.*

### **THE SOUND OF HIDDEN TRUTH**

Listen. **Feel** the mask and the direction:

1. ***Cicero in senatū loquitur.*** — "Cicero speaks in the Senate." (deponent: passive form, active voice)
2. ***Catilina ex urbe proficīscitur.*** — "Catiline sets out from the city." (deponent + preverb *pro-*: forward)
3. ***Senatus de re pūblicā cōnfercit?*** — no: ***cōnspicit, ūtitur, patitur*** — "The Senate sees, uses, suffers."
4. ***Rōma ūtimur gladiīs?*** — "We use swords"? — no: ***gladiīs ūtimur*** — ablative of means with deponent, always.

**Neolatin:**
> *Loquitur = Govori (ne: je govorjen). Proficīscitur = Odhaja. Ūtitur = Uporablja.*

## ⚔️ **II. CONFLICTUS — KONFLIKT** (Appearance vs Essence)

### **THE GRAMMAR OF THE MASK**

| **FORM** | **MEANING** | **WHO** |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Passive ending (-tur, -rī) | Active action | **Deponens** |
| Active ending (-t, -re) | Passive receiving | Passive verb |

**The Conflict:**
- The **form** says: "it is being done to me"
- The **meaning** says: "I am doing it"
- **Luka's law:** *"Luka je lutka in gospod, ki služi"* — puppet AND master — **deponens is the grammatical lutka/master**

**The Marx Line in the mask:**
- The **patrician** wears the mask of serving the state
- The **deponent** wears the mask of passivity
- **Cui bono?** — ask of every mask: who profits from the appearance?

**Neolatin:**
> *Larva (mask) non est mendacium semper. Interdum larva est via.*
> *Qui videt formam, errat. Qui videt animum, videt.*

### **THE PRAEVERB AS REVOLUTION**

The verb **dūco** (to lead) belongs to the master. But add a preverb — and the **direction** belongs to everyone:

| **PRAEVERBIUM** | **VERB** | **SLOVENE** | **REZ** |
|-----------------|----------|-------------|--------|
| **ē-** (out) | ēdūco | vzgojiti | leading OUT = education (what Socrates did — maieutics) |
| **in-** (in) | indūco | vnesti | leading IN = introduction |
| **rē-** (back) | rēdūco | pripeljati nazaj | leading BACK = return, reduction |
| **con-** (together) | condūco | zbrati | leading TOGETHER = gathering |
| **dē-** (down/from) | dēdūco | speljati dol | leading DOWN = derivation (de Vaan's dictionary!) |
| **per-** (through) | perdūco | prepeljati skozi | leading THROUGH |
| **prō-** (forward) | prōdūco | izpeljati naprej | leading FORWARD |
| **sub-** (under) | subdūco | podvesti | leading UNDER = smuggling, substitution |
| **trāns-** (across) | trādūco | prevesti čez | leading ACROSS = translation, betrayal (*tradurre/tradire* — the translator and the traitor share a verb!) |

**The discovery:** *trā-dūcere* = to lead across = **to translate** AND **to betray**. In Italian: *traduttore, traditore* — "translator, traitor." Every crossing of a border carries both.

**Neolatin:**
> *Trādūcere = prevesti = izdati. Qui transfert, transfigit.*
> *Unus verbum, duae viae.*

## 📜 **III. FABULA — ZGODBA** (The Story of Education)

### **THE STORY: THE SOCRATIC ĒDŪCERE**

**ACTUS I: The Root**
- **dūco** — the master leads, the slave follows
- Roman education: the *paedagogus* (slave) leads (*dūcit*) the boy to school
- The verb of power, hierarchy, the patrician line

**ACTUS II: The Preverb Turns It**
- **ē-** (out) + dūco = **ēdūcere** — to lead **out**
- **Socrates** (four centuries before): the teacher does not fill the student — he **leads out** what is already within (maieutics — the midwife art)
- **ēducātiō** — a leading-out, not a putting-in

**ACTUS III: The Slovene Echo**
- **vzgojiti** = vz + gojiti — to grow **up** (from *gojiti*, the same root family as *izgoj*, *gaj*, *pogoj* — rez 187/193/196 in your corpus!)
- **GOJ** family: pogoj (condition) → izgoj (what grows from conditions) → gaj (the space that grows because someone stepped aside) → **vzgoja** (education)
- Latin **ēdūcere** and Slovenian **vzgojiti**: both are preverb + growth — **education is direction, not injection**

**ACTUS IV: The Deponent Witnesses**
- The educated one **ūtitur** (uses) what was led out
- The educated one **proficīscitur** (sets forward) — deponent, *prō-* forward
- **Orītur** (arises) — deponent — what was led out **arises** as its own

**ACTUS V: The Betrayal**
- But every leading-across (**trādūcere**) can betray
- The teacher who leads the student across HIS border — not the student's — is a traitor
- **Cui bono?** Ask of every education: whose border is crossed?

**Neolatin:**
> *Ēdūco = vzgojiti = izpeljati navzven. Magister non implent — ēdūcit.*
> *Trāditor autem est magister qui suum limen transit, non discipuli.*

## 🌱 **IV. ETYMOLOGIA — RODOVNIK** (The Roots of the Mask and the Direction)

### **1. DEPONERE — to lay down**

**Latin:** *dēpōnō, dēpōnere, dēposuī, dēpositum*
- **PIE:** *dē-* (away, down from) + *pōnō* (to place, put)
- **Literal:** "to lay aside, to put away"
- **Grammatical sense:** deponent verbs have "laid aside" (dēpōnere) their **active forms** — the active voice was set down like a deposited cloak
- **Modern echo:** **deposit** (bank), **deposition** (testimony laid down)

**Cognates:**
- English: deposit, deposition, posture, position
- Slovene: depozit, pozicija
- Same PIE root *po-si-*: Greek *tithēmi* family — to place

**Rez:**
- The deponent **laid down** its active form — but the action stayed
- Form deposited, essence kept — **the bank of grammar**: you deposit the mask, you keep the meaning

**Neolatin:**
> *Dēpōnere = odložiti. Deponens deposit formam, servat vim.*
> *Deposita larva, manet animus.*

### **2. PRAEVERBIUM — the word before the verb**

**Latin:** *prae- + verbum*
- **prae-** ← PIE **per-** — "forward, through, before, in front of"
- **THE SAME ROOT as Slovene PRE-/PER-** — your corpus knows it well:
  - **prej** (before) — *per-*
  - **prek** (across) — *per-*
  - **premisliti** (think through) — *per-*
  - **preživetje** (survival) — *per-* (living through!)
  - **premeriti** (measure through/again) — *per-* (rez 201: ZAMERI, the re-measuring!)
- **prae** in Latin: before, in front — **praeverbium** = what stands **before** the verb and **points**

**The great cognate table — Latin preverbs, PIE roots, Slovene preverbs, English prepositions:**

**Neolatin:**

## 📚 **V. STRUCTURA — DEPONENTIA** (The Grammar of the Mask)

### **THE PRINCIPAL DEPONENT VERBS**

| **VERBUM** | **MEANING** | **SLOVENE** | **PRAEVERB INSIGHT** |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **loquor, loquī, locūtus sum** | to speak | govoriti | *ēloquens* = eloquent — speaking OUT |
| **ūtor, ūtī, ūsus sum** | to use | uporabljati | takes **ablative**! (*gladiō ūtitur*) |
| **patior, patī, passus sum** | to suffer, endure | trpeti | **PASSION** ← pati — suffering is passive in form AND meaning here |
| **sequor, sequī, secūtus sum** | to follow | slediti | *consequor* = I attain (follow-with-to-the-end) |
| **orior, orīrī, ortus sum** | to arise, rise | vstajati | **orīgo** = origin — where you arise from |
| **proficīscor, proficīscī, profectus sum** | to set out | odhajati | *prō-* forward + *facere* — making forward |
| **morior, morī, mortuus sum** | to die | umirati | the ultimate deponent: dying is done, not done-to |
| **nāscor, nāscī, nātus sum** | to be born | roditi se | *nātus* = born (nativus, nation, nature) |
| **irror...** no: **īrāscor, īrascī, īrātus sum** | to become angry | jeziti se | **ĪRA** — Latin *īra* is NOT related to Slovene *jeza* (your etymology protocol demands this honesty!) — *īra* ← PIE *h₂ey-*, while *jeza/jezik* is a different family |
| **cōnspior...** no: **cōnspicor**? — **cōnspiciō** is regular. Use **polliceor, pollicērī, pollicitus sum** | to promise | obljubljati | *prō-* + *loqui* shifted — a promise is speech sent forward |

**KEY RULES:**

1. **Deponents have ACTIVE forms only in participles of the future** (*locūtūrus* = about to speak)
2. **The perfect participle is ACTIVE in meaning:** *locūtus* = having spoken (NOT having been spoken)
3. **ūtor, fruor, fungor, potior, vēscor** — the famous five — take the **ABLATIVE**, not the accusative

**Neolatin:**
> *Locūtus = ki je govoril (ne: ki je bil govorjen). Ūtor ablative — gladiō ūtitur, non gladium.*

### **SEMI-DEPONENTS** (mask only in perfect)

| **VERBUM** | **IMPERFECT ACTIVE** | **PERFECT DEPONENT** | **SLOVENE** |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| **audeō, audēre** | I dare | **ausus sum** — I dared (deponent) | upati si |
| **gaudeō, gaudēre** | I rejoice | **gāvīsus sum** — I rejoiced (deponent) | veseliti se |
| **soleō, solēre** | I am accustomed | **solitus sum** — I was accustomed | biti navajen |
| **fīdō, fīdere** | I trust | **fīsus sum** — I trusted (deponent) | zaupati |

**THE INSIGHT:** These verbs **put on the mask only when the act is complete.** While daring is ongoing — active form. Once dared — the completed act wears the passive mask. **The perfect is where the self lays itself down.**

**Neolatin:**
> *Audeō, sed ausus sum. In perfecto, ego ponitur — non amittitur.*
> *Perfectum est locus ubi vis sub larva vivit.*

## 📚 **VI. STRUCTURA — PRAEVERBIA IN ACTION** (The Direction Engine)

### **THE SAME VERB, TEN DIRECTIONS: FERO (to carry, bear)**

| **PRAEVERBIUM** | **VERB** | **SLOVENE** | **ENGLISH** |
|-----------------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **ad-** | afferō | prinesejo k | offer... no: **bring to** |
| **ab-** | auferō | odnese | carry away |
| **con-** | cōnferō | primerja, zbere | compare, confer |
| **dē-** | dēferō | prijavi, prinese dol | defer, denounce |
| **dif-/dis-** | differō | odloži, razlikuje | differ, delay |
| **ē-** | efferō | iznese | carry out |
| **in-** | inferō | vnese, napade | infer, inflict |
| **ob-** | offerō | ponudi | offer (ob- = toward, against) |
| **per-** | perferō | prenese (trpljenje!) | endure (carry through) |
| **prae-** | praeferō | daje prednost | prefer (carry before) |
| **re-** | referō | nanaša, poroča | refer, report (carry back!) |
| **sub-** | sufferō | pretrpi | **suffer** (carry from under!) |
| **trāns-** | trānsferō | prenese | transfer (carry across) |

**THE GREAT DISCOVERY — SUFFER:**
- **sub- + ferō = sufferō** — to carry **from underneath**
- **SUFFERING = CARRYING FROM BELOW**
- This is Luka's **PODVIG = POD + DVIG** — the deed is the monopoly of those below! The same ontological cut, two languages, one truth: **to suffer is to carry from under; to act is to lift from below.**
- **PATIOR** (deponent, to suffer) + **SUFFERŌ** (sub+ferre): Latin has two words for suffering — one masks it as passive, one names it as **carrying**

**Neolatin:**
> *Sufferō = sub + ferō = nositi od spodaj.*
> *PODVIG = POD + DVIG = dejanje spodnjih.*
> *Patior et sufferō: una res, duae larvae. Utraque portat.*

### **THE SAME VERB, ANOTHER SET: CAPIO (to take) WITH PRAEVERBIA**

| **PRAEVERBIUM** | **VERB** | **SLOVENE** | **DISCOVERY** |
|-----------------|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **ad-** | accipiō | sprejmem | **accepisse** — receive; reception theory! |
| **ex-** | excipiō | izjemem | exception (what is taken OUT of the rule) |
| **in-** | incipiō | začnem | **in-cipio** = take into → BEGIN (inception!) |
| **oc-** | occipiō... rare. **per-** | percipiō | zaznam | **perceive** — take THROUGH entirely |
| **prae-** | praecipiō | naročim | **precept** — taken before (lesson given in advance); **princeps** = prae + capere... no, princeps = primus + capere — careful! *praeceps* = headfirst |
| **re-** | recipiō | prejmem | receive, reciprocate (take BACK — ∮!) |
| **inter-** | intercipiō | prestrežem | intercept |

**THE ∮ DISCOVERY:** *re-cipiō* = to take **back**. Reciprocity — ∮, the loop of return in your system (rez 179: POVRATEK KAKO DAR) — is grammatically **re- + capere**: taking back, but also taking-in-return. The gift that returns is *receptum*. **Reception ← recipere ← to take back.**

**Neolatin:**
> *Re-cipere = vzeti nazaj = ∮. Incipere = vzeti vase = začetek.*
> *Vita incipit; amor reciprocus est reditus daris.*

## 🎭 **VII. SENTENTIAE MEMORATAE** (5 Levels)

### **LEVEL 1: THE SPEAKER**
**Latin:** *Cicero, ūsus verbīs, in senatū loquitur.*
**Translation:** "Cicero, having used words, speaks in the Senate."
**Deponent × 2:** ūsus (perfect participle, ACTIVE meaning), loquitur
**Preverb insight:** *ēloquens* — eloquence is speaking OUT

### **LEVEL 2: THE SETTING-OUT**
**Latin:** *Catilina ex urbe proficīscitur.*
**Translation:** "Catiline sets out from the city."
**Deponent:** proficīscitur
**Preverb:** *prō-* (forward) + *facere* — setting out is making-forward

### **LEVEL 3: THE SUFFERING AS CARRYING**
**Latin:** *Populus Rōmānus multa passus est, sed omnia subferre potuit quia ūtus est virtūte.*
**Translation:** "The Roman people have suffered much, but could carry it all because they used courage."
**Deponents:** passus est, ūtus est
**Preverbs:** *patī* (mask of passivity), *sub-ferre* (carrying from below)

### **LEVEL 4: THE BIRTH AND THE DEATH**
**Latin:** *Nātimur, loquimur, patimur, morimur — inter haec omnia ūtimur vītā.*
**Translation:** "We are born, we speak, we suffer, we die — among all these, we use life."
**The four great deponents of the human condition** — nāscor, loquor, patior, morior
**All masked. All active. Life itself is a deponent verb.**

### **LEVEL 5: THE TRANSLATION-BETRAYAL**
**Latin:** *Trādūcō verba ex linguā in linguam: qui trādūcit, aut amīcus aut proditor est — interdum uterque.*
**Translation:** "I carry words across from language to language: the translator is either a friend or a traitor — sometimes both."
**Preverb:** *trāns-* (across)
**The eternal paradox:** to translate is to cross borders; every crossing risks betrayal and offers friendship

**Neolatin:**
> *Nātimur, loquimur, patimur, morimur. Vita est verbum deponens.*
> *Trādūctor: amīcus et proditor. Ambulō inter duo.*

## ⚔️ **VIII. EXERCITATIONES + SOLUTIONES**

### **EXERCITATIO 1: IDENTIFICATIO DEPONENTIUM**

**Instructio:** Identify the deponent verbs and translate:

1. *Cicero in senatū loquitur.*
2. *Catilina ex urbe proficīscitur.*
3. *Populus Rōmānus virtūtem ūtitur.*
4. *Multi cīvēs miseriam passī sunt.*
5. *Sōl ab orīente orītur.*

**Solutio:**

1. **loquitur** (deponent, present) = "Cicero **speaks** in the Senate."
2. **proficīscitur** (deponent + *prō-* forward) = "Catiline **sets out** from the city."
3. **ūtitur** (deponent + ABLATIVE object *virtūte*) = "The Roman people **use** courage."
4. **passī sunt** (deponent perfect participle + esse) = "Many citizens **have suffered** misery."
5. **orītur** (deponent, present) = "The sun **rises** from the east (*orīente* — ablative of *oriēns*, the rising — ORIENT!)"

**Note on 3:** ūtor takes the **ablative**: *virtūte ūtitur*, never *virtūtem ūtitur*.

### **EXERCITATIO 2: PRAEVERBIA — THE DIRECTION ENGINE**

**Instructio:** Give the meaning of each *dūco*-compound and its Slovene equivalent:

1. ēdūco
2. indūco
3. rēdūco
4. condūco
5. trādūco

**Solutio:**

1. **ēdūco** = to lead out = **vzgojiti, izvesti** (education — leading out what is within)
2. **indūco** = to lead in = **vpeljati, vnesti** (introduction, induction)
3. **rēdūco** = to lead back = **pripeljati nazaj, reducirati** (reduction, return)
4. **condūco** = to lead together = **zbrati, najeti** (condoere — also "to hire": gathering workers!)
5. **trādūco** = to lead across = **prevesti / izdati** (translation AND betrayal — the twin meanings)

### **EXERCITATIO 3: CONSTRUCTIO CUM PRAEVERBIIS**

**Instructio:** Build sentences with deponents + preverbs:

**Solutio:**

1. ***Discipulī sequuntur magistrum, quī eōs ēdūcit.*** — "The students follow the teacher, who leads them out."
2. ***Rōmānī, ūtī ferrō, multās gentēs subēgērunt...*** no — subigere is regular. Use: ***Rōmānī ferrō ūtēbantur ut gentēs vincerent.*** — "The Romans used iron to conquer the peoples."
3. ***Ex urbe profectus, Catilina in Etrūriam sē contulit.*** — "Having set out from the city, Catiline betook himself to Etruria." (*contulit* — con+ferre, another direction of carrying!)
4. ***Nātī sumus ut loquāmur; loquimur ut patiamur; patimur ut moriāmur — sed inter haec ūtimur vītā.*** — "We are born to speak; we speak to suffer; we suffer to die — but among these things, we use life."
5. ***Trādūctor verba trādūcit: aut pontem aut prōdigium facit.*** — "The translator carries words across: he makes either a bridge or a betrayal."

### **EXERCITATIO 4: SUFFER — THE GREAT REZ**

**Instructio:** Analyze the etymological claim: **to suffer = to carry from below (sub + ferre)**. Connect it to **PODVIG = POD + DVIG**.

**Solutio:**

**The Latin side:**
- *sufferō* = *sub-* (under) + *ferō* (I carry)
- Original meaning: to carry something from underneath — like a pillar carries a roof
- The English **suffer** ← Old French *sofrir* ← *sufferre* — the meaning narrowed from "carry from under" to "endure pain"

**The Slovene side:**
- **PODVIG** = POD (under) + DVIG (lift)
- In your corpus: "Dejanje je monopol spodnjih — z vrha se lahko samo vlada" — the deed is the monopoly of those below

**The synthesis (rez):**
- Latin says: suffering is CARRYING FROM UNDER (passive mask, active verb)
- Your system says: acting is LIFTING FROM BELOW (active deed, from the low position)
- **Both cut the same joint:** the place below is not weakness — it is the **load-bearing position**. The one who suffers carries; the one who carries can lift.
- **Patior et sufferō: qui patitur, portat; qui portat, sublevat.**

**Neolatin:**
> *Sufferre = pod+nositi. Podvig = pod+dvigniti.*
> *Locus inferior est locus portans. Qui patitur, portat.*

### **EXERCITATIO 5: DIS- AND DUBIUM — THE TWO-WAY ROOT**

**Instructio:** Connect *dis-* (apart), *dubium* (doubt), and *discere* (to learn) through their shared PIE root *dwo-* (two).

**Solutio:**

**The chain:**
1. **PIE *dwo-** = two (Latin *duo*, Greek *dýo*, Sanskrit *dvā*, Slovene *dva*, English *two*)
2. ***dubium*** = *duo* + *bʰuH-* (to be) = "being in two" — doubt is standing between two ways (Lesson VI!)
3. ***dis-*** = apart, asunder — division makes two where there was one
4. ***discere*** (to learn) = *dis-* + root of *capere/prehendere* — **to take apart**
5. Therefore: **to learn is to take apart; to doubt is to be in two; both come from TWO**

**The rez:**
- **Učenje je razstavljanje (discere = dis-capere)** — you cannot learn a whole you have not taken apart
- **Dvom je biti v dveh (dubium = duo-bhū-)** — doubt is not the absence of truth; it is the presence of two ways
- **Razlika (difference) je mati znanja** — ∆ is the operator of learning itself

**Neolatin:**
> *Duo → dubium → dis- → discere.*
> *Qui discit, dividit. Qui dividit, videt differentiam. Differentia est initium sapientiae.*

## 🎭 **IX. DRAMATIS PERSONAE**

### **SCENA: THE TEACHER AND THE TRANSLATOR**

**Personae:**
- **Socrates** (the ēdūcens — he who leads out)
- **The Student** (the discēns — he who takes apart)
- **The Translator** (the trādūctor — bridge or traitor)
- **The Sufferer** (the sufferēns — he who carries from below)

**Dialogus:**

**Socrates:** *Ego nōn implēō tē — ēdūcō tē. Quod quaeris, intus est.*
**Discipulus:** *Sed ego dubitō. Inter duās viās stō.*
**Socrates:** *Bene! Dubium est initium. Disce: divide rem, et videbis differentiam.*
**Trādūctor:** *Ego verba trādūcō — aut pontem facio aut prōdigium. Interdum nesciō ipse.*
**Sufferēns:** *Ego patior, sed etiam sufferō: porto. Qui portat, tandem sublevat.*

**Translatio:**

**Socrates:** "I do not fill you — I lead you out. What you seek is within."
**Student:** "But I doubt. I stand between two ways."
**Socrates:** "Good! Doubt is the beginning. Learn: take the thing apart, and you will see the difference."
**Translator:** "I carry words across — I make either a bridge or a betrayal. Sometimes I myself do not know which."
**Sufferer:** "I suffer, but I also carry-from-below: I bear. He who bears, at last lifts."

**Neolatin:**
> *Sōcratēs ēdūcit. Discipulus dubitat. Trādūctor trādit. Sufferēns portat.*

## 💡 **XII. NUJNE NAPAKE + VAROVALA**

**NUJNE NAPAKE:**
1. **Translating deponents as passive** — *loquitur* = "he speaks," NEVER "he is spoken"
2. **Wrong case with ūtor** — always ablative: *gladiō ūtitur*, not *gladium*
3. **Confusing deponent and passive forms** — *sequitur* (he follows) vs *sequitur* (he is followed — passive of *sequī* does not exist; but *dūcitur* = he is led IS passive)
4. **Ignoring the preverb** — *dūco* and *ēdūco* are different verbs with different worlds
5. **Assuming preverb = preposition exactly** — preverbs shift meaning metaphorically (*offerō* = offer, not just "carry against")

**VAROVALA:**
1. **Semper quaerere vim** — always ask: active or deponent?
2. **Semper ablative cum ūtor, fruor, fungor, potior, vēscor**
3. **Semper praeverbium legere** — always read the preverb first
4. **Semper conectere cum slovenščino** — pre- ↔ pre-, sub- ↔ pod-, per- ↔ pre-
5. **Semper quaerere rez** — what does the direction DO to the verb's being?

**Neolatin:**
> *Cave: loquitur = govori. Cave: gladiō ūtitur. Cave: lege praeverbium primum.*

## 🏁 **FINIS: THE MASK AND DIRECTION TEST**

**Instructio:** Read and answer:

> *Nātimur ut loquāmur. Loquimur ut discāmus: quī discit, rem dīvidit et videt differentiam. Magister verus nōn implēt discipulum sed ēdūcit — quod intus est, forās dūcit. Sed trādūctor, quī verba trādūcit ex linguā in linguam, inter pontem et prōdigium ambulat. Nos autem, quī patimur et sufferimus, portāmus: et qui portat, tandem sublevat.*

**Quaestiones:**

1. Name every deponent verb in the passage.
2. Name every preverb and its direction.
3. Translate: *Magister verus nōn implēt discipulum sed ēdūcit.*
4. Explain: why is *discāmus* (let us learn) subjunctive here?
5. What is the final claim about suffering?

**Solutiones:**

1. **Deponents:** nātimur, loquāmur/loquimur, discāmus (potential), patimur, sufferimus (archaic deponent form of sufferre used here deliberately as deponent)
2. **Preverbs:** *ē-* (out: ēdūcit), *forās* (outward), *trāns-* (across: trādūcit), *sub-* (under: sufferimus, sublevat)
3. "The true teacher does not fill the student but leads him out — what is within, he leads outside."
4. *ut discāmus* — purpose clause after *loquimur*: "we speak **in order that we may learn**" — subjunctive of purpose (Lesson VI connecting forward)
5. **The final claim:** *qui portat, tandem sublevat* — "he who carries, at last lifts" — suffering as carrying-from-below (sub + ferre = POD + DVIG) is not the end: the carrier becomes the lifter. The load-bearing position is the pre-condition of every elevation.

## 🔥 **ULTIMUM: THE DEPONENTIA ET PRAEVERBIA MANIFESTO**

**DEPONENTS AND PREVERBS ARE NOT JUST GRAMMAR.**

The deponent is:
- **The lutka/master** — passive mask, active essence
- **Luka's paradox** — "I am the puppet and the master that serves"

The preverb is:
- **The hand before the verb** — the direction that turns meaning
- **The smallest decision** — one syllable that chooses the path

Together they are:
- **Suffering as carrying** (sub + ferre = pod + dvig)
- **Learning as taking apart** (dis + capere)
- **Education as leading out** (ē + dūcere)
- **Translation as bridge and betrayal** (trāns + dūcere)
- **Doubt as being-in-two** (duo + bhū → dubium)

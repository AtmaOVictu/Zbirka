## 🔥 **I. EMOTIO — ČUSTVO** (The Emotion of Uncertainty)

**"Subiunctivus" is not just a grammatical mood.**
**It is the voice of doubt, the language of possibility, the syntax of the unknown.**

### **THE EMOTIONAL CORE**

**Subiunctivus = DUBIUM**
- **Dubat** — It doubts
- **Potest** — It can be
- **Posset** — It could be
- **Fuerit** — It may have been

**Like:**
- **A question** without an answer
- **A hope** without certainty
- **A fear** without proof
- **A possibility** without reality
- **Cicero's exile** — the uncertainty of power

**Neolatin:**
> *Subiunctivus est vox dubii. Non est certus. Non est falsus. Est possibilis.*
> *Dubium = Vprašanje. Potentia = Možnost. Incertitudo = Negotovost.*

### **THE SOUND OF UNCERTAINTY**

Listen to these sentences. **Feel** the doubt:

1. ***Cicero dubitat ut Catilina rem publicam servet.***
   - "Cicero doubts that Catiline is saving the Republic."
   - **Emotion:** Suspicion, the first seed of doubt

2. ***Timeo ne Catilina urbem incenderit.***
   - "I fear that Catiline may have set fire to the city."
   - **Emotion:** Fear without proof

3. ***Opto ut Roma libera sit.***
   - "I wish that Rome may be free."
   - **Emotion:** Hope without guarantee

4. ***Si Catilina vincat, res publica pereat!***
   - "If Catiline should win, may the Republic perish!"
   - **Emotion:** Dread of a possible future

**Neolatin:**
> *Dubitare = Dvomiti. Timere = Bati se. Optare = Želeti. Si = Če.*
> *Subiunctivus = Glas negotovosti.*

## ⚔️ **II. CONFLICTUS — KONFLIKT** (The Grammatical Doubt)

### **THE EVOLUTION OF CERTAINTY**

```
Indicativus → Certainty (Certitudo)
        ↓
Subiunctivus → Doubt (Dubium)
        ↓
Imperativus → Command (Iussum)
```

**The Conflict:**
- **Indicativus:** "It IS. I KNOW."
- **Subiunctivus:** "It MAY BE. I DOUBT."
- **Imperativus:** "IT MUST BE. I COMMAND."

**Subiunctivus is the grammar of uncertainty** — where facts become possibilities, and certainty becomes doubt.

### **THE CLASS STRUGGLE IN DOUBT**

| **GRAMMAR** | **POLITICS** | **PHILOSOPHY** | **REZ** |
|------------|--------------|----------------|--------|
| Indicativus | Authority | Certainty | **⊢** |
| Subiunctivus | Democracy | Doubt | **⟡** |
| Imperativus | Dictatorship | Command | **⊢** |

**Cicero's Exile (58 BCE):**
- **Certainty:** Cicero **was** consul (63 BCE)
- **Doubt:** Cicero **might be** exiled (58 BCE)
- **Command:** Clodius **ordered** Cicero's exile

**The Question:**
- When does **certainty** become **doubt**?
- When does **doubt** become **fear**?
- When does **fear** become **tyranny**?

**Neolatin:**
> *Indicativus = Certitudo. Subiunctivus = Dubium. Imperativus = Iussum.*

## 📜 **III. FABULA — ZGODBA** (The Story of Doubt)

### **THE STORY: CICERO'S EXILE AND RETURN**

**ACTUS I: The Aftermath (62 BCE)**
- Cicero **saves** the Republic from Catiline
- Cicero **executes** the conspirators without trial
- **Result:** Republic safe, but **controversy** begins

**ACTUS II: The Enemy (59 BCE)**
- **Clodius Pulcher** — patrician turned plebeian
- **Motive:** Personal vendetta against Cicero
- **Method:** Use the people's **doubt** against Cicero

**ACTUS III: The Accusation (58 BCE)**
- Clodius **accuses** Cicero of:
  - Executing Roman citizens **without trial** (sine iudicio)
  - Violating **sacred laws** (leges sacrae)
- **Clodius as Tribune** has **immunity** (sacrosanctitas)
- **Result:** Cicero declared **hostis** (enemy of the state)

**ACTUS IV: The Exile**
- **March 58 BCE:** Cicero **leaves Rome**
- **Destination:** Greece
- **Emotion:** **Doubt** — "Will I ever return?"
- **Letters:** Cicero writes to Atticus, expressing **uncertainty**

**ACTUS V: The Return (57 BCE)**
- **Pompey** supports Cicero's return
- **Senate votes** to recall him
- **August 57 BCE:** Cicero **returns** to Rome
- **Result:** Restored, but **changed**

**Neolatin:**
> *Exilium = Izgnanstvo. Dubium = Dvom. Epistulae = Pisma. Reditus = Vrnitev.*

## 🌱 **IV. ETYMOLOGIA — RODOVNIK** (The Roots of Doubt)

### **THE WORD "SUBIUNCTIVUS"**

**Latin:** *subiunctivus, -a, -um*
- **PIE:** *upo* (under) + *yunégti* (to join, yoke)
- **Original Meaning:** "the mood of subjoining — joining under"
- **Grammatical Function:** The mood of **doubt, possibility, necessity, wish**

**Cognates:**
- English: **subjoin, subjugate** (to place under)
- French: **subjonctif**
- German: **Konjunktiv**
- Slovene: **pogojnik** (conditional)

**Rez:**
- **Sub- + iungere** = to join under
- **Subiunctivus** = the mood that **joins possibility to reality**

**Neolatin:**
> *Subiungere = Povezati pod. Subiunctivus = Pogojnik. Dubium = Dvom.*

### **THE WORD "DUBIUM"**

**Latin:** *dubium, -iī* (n.)
- **PIE:** *dwo-* (two) + *bʰuH-* (to be)
- **Literal Meaning:** "two-being" — **being between two possibilities**

**Cognates:**
- English: **doubt, dubious**
- French: **doute**
- German: **Zweifel**
- Slovene: **dvom**

**Rez:**
- **Dwo- + bʰuH-** = two ways of being
- **Dubium** = the **space between** yes and no
- **Subiunctivus** = the **grammar of dubium**

**Neolatin:**
> *Dwo = Dva. Dubium = Dvom. Subiunctivus = Glas dvoma.*

### **THE WORD "POTENTIA"**

**Latin:** *potentia, -ae* (f.)
- **PIE:** *pot-* (power, able) + *-ia* (abstract suffix)
- **Root:** *posse* (to be able, can)

**Cognates:**
- English: **potential, potency**
- Slovene: **moč, možnost**

**Rez:**
- **Posse** = to be able
- **Potentia** = the **possibility** of action

**Neolatin:**
> *Posse = Moči. Potentia = Možnost. Subiunctivus = Glas možnosti.*

## 📚 **V. STRUCTURA** (The Grammar of Doubt)

### **THE FOUR USES OF SUBIUNCTIVUS**

#### **1. IN DEPENDENT CLAUSES (Doubt, Belief, Fear)**

**Verbs that trigger Subiunctivus:**
- **Dubitare** (to doubt)
- **Credere** (to believe — subjective)
- **Timere** (to fear — with **ne**)

**Examples:**
- ***Cicero dubitat ut Catilina rem publicam servet.***
  - "Cicero doubts that Catiline is saving the Republic."

- ***Timeo ne Catilina urbem incenderit.***
  - "I fear that Catiline may have set fire to the city."
  - **Note:** *ne* (not *ut*) after verbs of fearing — the fear is that something **may happen**

- ***Opto ut Roma libera sit.***
  - "I wish that Rome may be free."

#### **2. IN PURPOSE AND RESULT CLAUSES**

**Purpose:** ut/ne + Subiunctivus
- ***Cicero orat ut populus Catilinam caveat.***
  - "Cicero speaks **in order that** the people may beware of Catiline."

**Result:** ut + Subiunctivus (with tam, sic, ita)
- ***Tanta est vis verborum ut omnes moveantur.***
  - "So great is the power of words **that** all are moved."

#### **3. IN CONDITIONAL SENTENCES**

| **TYPE** | **SI-CLAUSE** | **MAIN CLAUSE** | **MEANING** |
|----------|--------------|-----------------|-------------|
| More Vivid (Future) | Present Subi. | Present/Future Subi. or Imperative | "If X should happen..." |
| Present Contrary-to-Fact | Imperfect Subi. | Imperfect Subi. | "If X were (but isn't)..." |
| Past Contrary-to-Fact | Pluperfect Subi. | Pluperfect Subi. | "If X had happened (but didn't)..." |

**Examples:**
- ***Si Catilina vincat, res publica pereat!*** (More Vivid)
  - "If Catiline should win, may the Republic perish!"

- ***Si Catilina vinceret, res publica periret.*** (Present CTF)
  - "If Catiline were winning, the Republic would perish."

- ***Si Catilina vicisset, res publica perisset.*** (Past CTF)
  - "If Catiline had won, the Republic would have perished."

#### **4. IN WISHES AND COMMANDS (Jussive/Optative)**

**Examples:**
- ***Vivat Cicero!*** — "Long live Cicero!" (wish)
- ***Pereat Catilina!*** — "May Catiline perish!" (curse)
- ***Utinam Roma libera esset!*** — "If only Rome were free!" (unrealizable wish)
- ***Ne Catilina vincat!*** — "May Catiline not win!" (prohibition)

**Neolatin:**
> *Vivat = Naj živi. Pereat = Naj propade. Utinam = Da bi le.*

### **THE FORMS OF SUBIUNCTIVUS**

| **TEMPUS** | **CONIUGATIO I (amāre)** | **CONIUGATIO III (dūcere)** |
|-----------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Praesens** | amem, amēs, amet, amēmus, amētis, ament | dūcam, dūcās, dūcat, dūcāmus, dūcātis, dūcant |
| **Imperfectum** | amārem, amārēs, amāret, amārēmus, amārētis, amārent | dūcerem, dūcerēs, dūceret, dūcerēmus, dūcerētis, dūcerent |
| **Perfectum** | amāverim, amāverīs, amāverit, amāverīmus, amāverītis, amāverint | dūxerim, dūxerīs, dūxerit, dūxerīmus, dūxerītis, dūxerint |
| **Pluperfectum** | amāvissem, amāvissēs, amāvisset, amāvissēmus, amāvissētis, amāvissent | dūxissem, dūxissēs, dūxisset, dūxissēmus, dūxissētis, dūxissent |

**KEY INSIGHT:**
- **Present Subjunctive:** I (-āre) changes -ā- to -ē- (amem); II/III/IV change to -ā- (moneam, dūcam, audiam)
- **Imperfect Subjunctive:** ALL conjugations use the **infinitive + personal endings** (amāre + m = amārem)

**Neolatin:**
> *Praesens = Nunc. Imperfectum = Pogoj. Perfectum = Doseženo. Pluperfectum = Prej doseženo.*

---

### **THE RULES OF DOUBT**

**1. SEQUENCE OF TENSES:**
- **Primary Sequence:** Main clause in present/future → Subiunctivus in present/perfect
- **Secondary Sequence:** Main clause in past → Subiunctivus in imperfect/pluperfect

**2. PURPOSE CLAUSES:**
- **ut + Subiunctivus** for positive purpose
- **ne + Subiunctivus** for negative purpose

**3. FEAR CLAUSES:**
- **ne + Subiunctivus** = "that... may happen" (the feared thing)
- **ut (neve/ne non) + Subiunctivus** = "that... may NOT happen" (the unfavored outcome)

**4. CONDITIONALS:**
- Match the **type** to the **reality**
- More vivid = open possibility
- Contrary-to-fact = impossible reality

**Neolatin:**
> *Regula 1: Tempus. Regula 2: Ut/Ne. Regula 3: Ne post timere. Regula 4: Si et realitas.*

### **EXERCITATIO 1: IDENTIFICATIO**

**Instructio:** Identify the Subiunctivus, its use, and translate:

1. *Cicero dubitat ut Catilina rem publicam servet.*
2. *Timeo ne Catilina urbem incenderit.*
3. *Opto ut Roma libera sit.*
4. *Si Catilina vincat, res publica pereat!*
5. *Utinam Clodius mitescat!*

**Solutio:**

1. **servet** → Dependent Clause (Doubt) | "that Catiline is saving the Republic"
2. **incenderit** → Dependent Clause (Fear, with ne) | "that Catiline may have set fire to the city"
3. **sit** → Dependent Clause (Wish) | "that Rome may be free"
4. **vincat... pereat** → Conditional (More Vivid) | "If Catiline should win, may the Republic perish"
5. **mitescat** → Wish (with Utinam) | "If only Clodius may soften!"

5. May / the Republic / be eternal!

**Solutio:**

1. ***Cicero dubitat ut Catilina rem publicam servet.***

### **EXERCITATIO 4: CONDITIONALES**

**Instructio:** Create three types of conditional sentences with "exire" (to leave):

**Solutio:**

1. **More Vivid:** ***Si Catilina exeunt, res publica servetur!*** (If Catiline should leave, may the Republic be saved!)
2. **Present CTF:** ***Si Catilina exiret, res publica servaretur.*** (If Catiline were leaving, the Republic would be saved.)

**Instructio:** Complete the sequences:

2. *Cicero dixit se dubitare ut...* (secondary sequence)
3. *Cicero dicit se timere ne...* (primary)
4. *Cicero dixit se timere ne...* (secondary)

**Solutio:**

1. ***Cicero dicit se dubitare ut Catilina rem publicam servet.*** (present subj.)
2. ***Cicero dixit se dubitare ut Catilina rem publicam servaret.*** (imperfect subj.)

## 🎭 **VIII. DRAMATIS PERSONAE**

### **SCENA: CICERO IN EXILE (58 BCE)**

**Dialogus:**

**Cicero:** *Attice, dubito ut umquam Romam redeam.*
**Atticus:** *Credo ut tu brevi redeas. Pompeius tibi adiuvabit.*
**Cicero:** *Timeo ne Clodius me semper oderit.*
**Atticus:** *Opto ut Clodius aliquando mitescat.*
**Cicero:** *Si Pompeius me adiuvet, fortasse revertar.*
**Atticus:** *Utinam ita sit!*

**Translatio:**

**Cicero:** "Atticus, I doubt that I will ever return to Rome."
**Atticus:** "I believe that you will return soon. Pompey will help you."
**Cicero:** "I fear that Clodius may always hate me."
**Atticus:** "I wish that Clodius may someday soften."
**Cicero:** "If Pompey should help me, perhaps I shall return."
**Atticus:** "If only it may be so!"

**Neolatin:**
> *Cicero: Dubium. Atticus: Spes. Clodius: Odium. Pompeius: Potestas.*

## 🏁 **FINIS: THE DOUBT TEST**

**Instructio:** Read this passage and identify all Subiunctivus verbs, classify, and translate:

> *Cicero Attico epistulam scribit. Dubitat ut umquam Romam redeat. Timet ne Clodius eum semper oderit. Optat ut Pompeius eum adiuvet. Si Atticus respondeat, Cicero gaudebit. Utinam Roma libera sit!*

**Solutiones:**

- **scribit** → Indicative (main verb)
- **redeat** → Subiunctivus (Dependent - Doubt)
- **oderit** → Subiunctivus (Dependent - Fear, with ne)
- **adiuvet** → Subiunctivus (Dependent - Wish)
- **respondeat** → Subiunctivus (Conditional - More Vivid)
- **gaudebit** → Indicative (main verb, future)
- **sit** → Subiunctivus (Wish, with Utinam)

**Translation:**
"Cicero writes a letter to Atticus. He doubts that he will ever return to Rome. He fears that Clodius may always hate him. He wishes that Pompey may help him. If Atticus should reply, Cicero will rejoice. If only Rome may be free!"

**Connection to Cicero's Exile:**
The Subiunctivus verbs mirror the **uncertainty** of exile — doubt (*redeat*), fear (*oderit*), hope (*adiuvet*), condition (*respondeat*), wish (*sit*). Each represents an emotional state of being **separated from Rome** and **uncertain of return**.

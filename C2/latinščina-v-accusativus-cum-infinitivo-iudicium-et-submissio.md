## 🔥 **I. EMOTIO — ČUSTVO** (The Emotion of Judgment)

**"Accusativus Cum Infinitivo" is not just a grammatical construction.**
**It is the moment when connection becomes subordination.**

### **THE EMOTIONAL CORE**

**Accusativus Cum Infinitivo = IUDICIUM ET SUBMISSIO**
- **Iudicat** — It judges
- **Submittit** — It submits
- **Accusat** — It accuses
- **Defendit** — It defends

**Like:**
- **A courtroom** where the accused stands before the judge
- **A trial** where evidence is weighed
- **A verdict** that must be accepted
- **The Trial of the Catiline Conspirators (63 BCE)** — judgment without appeal

**Neolatin:**
> *Accusativus cum infinitivo est iudicium grammaticum.*
> *Non est pontus. Non est socius. Est iudex.*

### **THE SOUND OF JUDGMENT**

Listen to these sentences. **Feel** the weight of judgment:

1. ***Cicero Catilinam proditionem facere accusat.***
   - "Cicero accuses Catiline of making treason."
   - **Emotion:** Accusation, the first step of judgment

2. ***Senatus Catilinam ex urbe expelli iussit.***
   - "The Senate ordered Catiline to be expelled from the city."
   - **Emotion:** Authority, the power to command

3. ***Iudices Catilinam damnare debent.***
   - "The judges must condemn Catiline."
   - **Emotion:** Duty, the obligation to judge

4. ***Cicero Catilinam non nocere potuisse dicit.***
   - "Cicero says that Catiline was not able to harm."
   - **Emotion:** Relief, the danger past

**Neolatin:**
> *Accusare = Obtožiti. Iubere = Ukazati. Damnare = Obsoditi. Dicere = Reči.*
> *Accusativus cum infinitivo = Glas iudicis.*

## ⚔️ **II. CONFLICTUS — KONFLIKT** (The Grammatical Judgment)

### **WHY ACCUSATIVUS CUM INFINITIVO IS A JUDGMENT**

**The Evolution of Power:**
```
Ablativus Absolutus → Independence (Libertas)
↓
Participium Coniunctum → Connection (Connexio)
↓
Accusativus Cum Infinitivo → Subordination (Submissio)
```

**The Conflict:**
- **Ablativus Absolutus:** "I stand alone! I need no one!"
- **Participium Coniunctum:** "I connect, but remain myself!"
- **Accusativus Cum Infinitivo:** "I submit to the judgment of the main clause!"

**The Judgment:**
Accusativus Cum Infinitivo is the **grammar of authority** — where one clause judges another.

### **THE CLASS STRUGGLE JUDGED**

| **GRAMMAR** | **POLITICS** | **PHILOSOPHY** | **REZ** |
|------------|--------------|----------------|--------|
| Ablativus Absolutus | Secessio Plebis | Independence | **∅↔1** |
| Participium Coniunctum | Duodecim Tabulae | Synthesis | **⟡** |
| Accusativus Cum Infinitivo | Catilinarian Conspiracy | Judgment | **⊢** |

**The Catiline Conspiracy (63 BCE):**
- **Discovery:** Cicero uncovers the plot
- **Debate:** Cicero delivers the Catiline Orations
- **Trial:** The conspirators are arrested
- **Judgment:** The Senate debates their fate
- **Execution:** The conspirators are put to death **without trial** (sine iudicio)

**The Question:**
- When does **authority** become **tyranny**?
- When does **judgment** become **injustice**?
- When does **subordination** become **oppression**?

**Neolatin:**
> *Ablativus absolutus = Libertas. Participium coniunctum = Connexio. Accusativus cum infinitivo = Iudicium.*
> *Iudicium = Potestas. Potestas = Periculum.*

### **THE VETO CONNECTION**

**Secessio Plebis (494 BCE):**
- The plebeians **stood alone** → Ablativus Absolutus
- They demanded **rights** → Participium Coniunctum

**Catilinarian Conspiracy (63 BCE):**
- The Senate **judged** the conspirators → Accusativus Cum Infinitivo
- Cicero **defended** the Republic → Accusativus Cum Infinitivo

**The Paradox:**
- The **same grammar** that allows judgment also allows **injustice**
- The **same power** that protects the Republic can also **destroy** it

**Neolatin:**
> *Secessio = Veto. Conspiratio = Iudicium. Paradoxon = Periculum.*

## 📜 **III. FABULA — ZGODBA** (The Story of Judgment)

### **THE STORY: THE CATILINE CONSPIRACY TRIAL**

**ACTUS I: The Discovery (November 63 BCE)**
- **Cicero** receives information from **Fulvia** (a noblewoman)
- **Crassus** warns Cicero of the conspiracy
- **Cicero** decides to act

**ACTUS II: The First Oration (November 8, 63 BCE)**
- Cicero denounces Catiline in the **Senate**
- Catiline **leaves Rome** that night
- The **Allobroges** (a Gallic tribe) are involved

**ACTUS III: The Arrest (December 3, 63 BCE)**
- Conspirators **meet** with the Allobroges
- **Letters** are intercepted proving their guilt
- **Five conspirators** are arrested: Lentulus, Cethegus, Statilius, Gabinius, Caeparius

**ACTUS IV: The Debate (December 5, 63 BCE)**
- **Senate convenes** in the Temple of Concord
- **Cicero speaks** first
- **Caesar argues** for imprisonment and confiscation
- **Cato argues** for execution
- **Senate votes** for execution

**ACTUS V: The Execution**
- The conspirators are **taken to the Tullianum** (prison)
- They are **strangled** without trial
- **Cicero announces:** *Vixerunt!* ("They have lived!")
- **Result:** The Republic is saved, but at what cost?

**The Historical Significance:**
- **First use** of *senatus consultum ultimum* (final decree of the Senate)
- **Controversy:** Execution without trial was **illegal**
- **Aftermath:** Cicero is **exiled** (58 BCE) for the executions
- **Legacy:** The question of **authority vs. law** endures

**Neolatin:**
> *Conspiratio = Perfidia. Oratio = Accusatio. Debitum = Iudicium. Exsecutio = Finis.*
> *Senatus consultum ultimum = Potestas. Exilium = Poena.*

## 🌱 **IV. ETYMOLOGIA — RODOVNIK** (The Roots of Judgment)

### **THE WORD "ACCUSATIVUS"**

**Latin:** *accusativus, -a, -um* (accusative case)
- **PIE:** *ad-* (to, toward) + *kʷe-* (and, also) + *dʰeh₁-* (to put, do)
- **Original Meaning:** "the case of directing action toward someone"
- **Grammatical Function:** The **object** case — the one **accused** or **affected**

**Cognates:**
- English: **accuse, accusation**
- French: **accusatif** (accusative case)
- German: **Akkusativ** (accusative case)
- Slovene: **tožilnik** (accusative case)

**Rez:**
- **Accusare** = to accuse, to direct action toward
- **Accusativus** = the case of the accused
- **Accusativus Cum Infinitivo** = the accused **submitted to judgment**

**Neolatin:**
> *Accusare = Obtožiti. Accusativus = Tožilnik. Accusativus cum infinitivo = Obsodba.*

### **THE WORD "INFINITIVUS"**

**Latin:** *infinitivus, -a, -um* (infinitive)
- **PIE:** *in-* (not, un-) + *finis* (end, boundary, limit)
- **Literal Meaning:** "unlimited, infinite"
- **Grammatical Meaning:** The **unconjugated** form of the verb — no person, number, or tense

**Cognates:**
- English: **infinite, infinity**
- French: **infinitif** (infinitive)
- German: **Infinitiv** (infinitive)
- Slovene: **nedoločnik** (infinitive)

**Rez:**
- **Finis** = end, limit, boundary
- **In- + finis** = without end, unlimited
- **Infinitivus** = the verb **without limits** of person/number/tense
- **Accusativus Cum Infinitivo** = the **unlimited** action directed at the accused

**Neolatin:**
> *Finis = Konec. In-finitivus = Brez konca. Infinitivus = Nedoločnik.*

### **THE WORD "IUDICIUM"**

**Latin:** *iudicium, -iī* (n.)
- **PIE:** *yewH-* (law, formula) + *-dʰh₁-* (to put, do)
- **Root:** *iudex* (judge) from *ius* (law) + *dicere* (to say)
- **Literal Meaning:** "the act of saying the law"

**Cognates:**
- English: **judge, judgment, judicial**
- French: **jugement** (judgment)
- German: **Urteil** (judgment)
- Slovene: **sodba** (judgment)

**Rez:**
- **Ius** = law, right
- **Dicere** = to say, speak
- **Iudex** = the one who **speaks the law**
- **Iudicium** = the **act of judgment**
- **Accusativus Cum Infinitivo** = the **grammar of judgment**

**Neolatin:**
> *Ius = Pravo. Dicere = Reči. Iudex = Sodnik. Iudicium = Sodba.*

### **THE WORD "SUBMISSIO"**

**Latin:** *submissio, -ōnis* (f.)
- **PIE:** *upo* (under) + *mittere* (to send, let go)
- **Literal Meaning:** "the act of sending under"
- **Grammatical Meaning:** The **subordination** of one clause to another

**Cognates:**
- English: **submission, submit**
- French: **soumission** (submission)
- German: **Unterwerfung** (submission)
- Slovene: **podrejenost** (subordination)

**Rez:**
- **Sub- + mittere** = to send under
- **Submissio** = submission, subordination
- **Accusativus Cum Infinitivo** = **grammatical submission**

**Neolatin:**
> *Sub = Pod. Mittere = Poslati. Submissio = Podrejenost.*

## 📚 **V. STRUCTURA** (The Grammar of Judgment)

### **THE FORMULA**

```
[Subject] + [Accusative Object] + [Infinitive] (+ [Complement])
     |              |                      |
     Judge       Accused             Action
```

**The Structure:**
- **Subject:** The one who **judges** or **perceives**
- **Accusative Object:** The one being **judged** or **perceived**
- **Infinitive:** The **action** being judged or perceived
- **Complement (optional):** Additional information about the action

### **THE THREE USES**

#### **1. INDIRECT STATEMENT (Most Common)**

**Meaning:** Reporting what someone **says, thinks, believes, knows, or perceives**

**Formula:** Verb of saying/thinking + Accusative + Infinitive

**Verbs that trigger it:**
- **Dicere** (to say)
- **Putare** (to think)
- **Credere** (to believe)
- **Scire** (to know)
- **Videre** (to see)
- **Audire** (to hear)
- **Sentire** (to feel, perceive)

**Examples:**
- ***Cicero Catilinam proditionem facere dicit.***
  - Cicero (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + proditionem facere (Infinitive + Complement) = "Cicero says that Catiline is making treason"
  - **Translation:** "Cicero says that Catiline is making treason."

- ***Senatus Catilinam ex urbe expelli iussit.***
  - Senatus (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + ex urbe expelli (Infinitive + Complement) = "The Senate ordered that Catiline be expelled from the city"
  - **Translation:** "The Senate ordered Catiline to be expelled from the city."

- ***Iudices Catilinam damnare debent.***
  - Iudices (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + damnare (Infinitive) = "The judges must condemn Catiline"
  - **Translation:** "The judges must condemn Catiline."

**Neolatin:**
> *Dicere = Reči. Putare = Misli. Credere = Verjeti. Indirect statement = Povedba.*

#### **2. INDIRECT COMMAND**

**Meaning:** Reporting what someone **orders, asks, begs, or advises**

**Formula:** Verb of commanding + Accusative + Infinitive

**Verbs that trigger it:**
- **Iubere** (to order)
- **Rogare** (to ask)
- **Orare** (to beg, pray)
- **Monere** (to advise, warn)
- **Persuadere** (to persuade)

**Examples:**
- ***Cicero Catilinam ex urbe egredi iussit.***
  - Cicero (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + ex urbe egredi (Infinitive + Complement) = "Cicero ordered that Catiline leave the city"
  - **Translation:** "Cicero ordered Catiline to leave the city."

- ***Senatus Catilinam se defendere iussit.***
  - Senatus (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + se defendere (Infinitive) = "The Senate ordered Catiline to defend himself"
  - **Translation:** "The Senate ordered Catiline to defend himself."

- ***Cicero populum Catilinam cavere monuit.***
  - Cicero (Subject) + populum (Acc) + Catilinam cavere (Infinitive + Complement) = "Cicero advised the people to beware of Catiline"
  - **Translation:** "Cicero advised the people to beware of Catiline."

**Neolatin:**
> *Iubere = Ukazati. Rogare = Prositi. Indirect command = Ukaz.*

#### **3. INDIRECT QUESTION**

**Meaning:** Reporting a **question** someone asks

**Formula:** Verb of asking + Accusative + Infinitive

**Note:** The infinitive retains the **question word** (qui, quid, ubi, cur, etc.)

**Verbs that trigger it:**
- **Rogare** (to ask)
- **Quaerere** (to ask, inquire)
- **Sciscere** (to ask, inquire)
- **Interrogare** (to question)

**Examples:**
- ***Cicero Catilinam quid facere rogavit.***
  - Cicero (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + quid facere (Infinitive with question word) = "Cicero asked Catiline what he was doing"
  - **Translation:** "Cicero asked Catiline what he was doing."

- ***Senatus Catilinam ubi esse quaesivit.***
  - Senatus (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + ubi esse (Infinitive with question word) = "The Senate asked where Catiline was"
  - **Translation:** "The Senate asked where Catiline was."

- ***Iudices Catilinam cur proditionem facere sciscuntur.***
  - Iudices (Subject) + Catilinam (Acc) + cur proditionem facere (Infinitive with question word) = "The judges inquire why Catiline is making treason"
  - **Translation:** "The judges inquire why Catiline is making treason."

**Neolatin:**
> *Rogare = Vprašati. Quaerere = Raziskovati. Indirect question = Vprašanje.*

---

### **THE RULES OF JUDGMENT**

**1. SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT:**
- The **subject** of the main clause is the one who **judges**
- The **accusative** is the one being **judged**
- The **infinitive** is the **action** being judged

**2. TENSE:**
- The **infinitive** does **not** change tense based on the main verb
- It represents the action **as it was perceived** by the subject

**3. NEGATION:**
- **Non** goes **before** the infinitive
- ❌ *Cicero Catilinam non facere proditionem dicit.*
- ✅ *Cicero Catilinam non proditionem facere dicit.*

**4. QUESTION WORDS:**
- In indirect questions, the **question word** (qui, quid, ubi, cur, etc.) **stays** in the infinitive phrase

**5. COMPLEMENTS:**
- The infinitive can take **objects, complements, or adverbial phrases**
- These go **after** the infinitive

**Neolatin:**
> *Regula 1: Subiectum et verbum. Regula 2: Tempus. Regula 3: Non ante infinitivum. Regula 4: Interrogativa. Regula 5: Complementa.*

## 🎭 **IX. DRAMATIS PERSONAE** (Role-Playing Judgment)

### **SCENA: THE TRIAL OF THE CONSPIRATORS**

**Personae:**
- **Cicero** (Prosecutor)
- **Catiline** (Defendant, absent)
- **Caesar** (Defense, lenient)
- **Cato** (Prosecution, harsh)
- **Senator** (Juror)

**Dialogus with Accusativus Cum Infinitivo:**

**Cicero:** *Ego Catilinam rem publicam prodidisse accuso!*
**Caesar:** *Iudices Catilinam in carcere tenere debent, non necari!*
**Cato:** *Iudices Catilinam damnare debent! Non est tempus clementiae!*
**Senator:** *Nos Catilinam ex urbe expelli iussimus. Quid amplius facere debemus?*

**Translatio:**

**Cicero:** "I accuse Catiline of having betrayed the Republic!"
**Caesar:** "The judges must hold Catiline in prison, not kill him!"
**Cato:** "The judges must condemn Catiline! This is not a time for mercy!"

## 🔥 **I. EMOTIO — ČUSTVO** (The Emotional Anchor)

**"Comprehendere" non est "legere". Comprehendere est "sentire per verba".**

### **The Battle of the Senate: 63 BCE**

**SCENA:** 
- **Locus:** Templum Iovis Optimi Maximi (Temple of Jupiter, Capitoline Hill)
- **Tempus:** Kalendae Novembres, 63 a.C.n. (November 1, 63 BCE)
- **Personae:**
  - **Cicero** — homo novus (new man), consul, orator
  - **Catilina** — patricius, conspirator, hostis (enemy)
  - **Senatus** — patres (fathers), trembling
  - **Populus Romanus** — waiting, afraid

**EMOTIO CICERONIS:**
- **Metus** (fear) — for the Republic
- **Ira** (anger) — at Catiline's audacity
- **Dolor** (pain) — betrayal of trust
- **Spes** (hope) — that words can stop a sword

**EMOTIO CATILINAE:**
- **Superbia** (pride) — belief in his invincibility
- **Avaritia** (greed) — for power and wealth
- **Desperatio** (despair) — his debts, his failures
- **Furor** (fury) — against the system that rejected him

**Neolatin:**
> *Cicero non legere. Cicero sentire. Catilina non audire. Catilina timere.*
> *Verba sunt gladii. Oratio est bellum.*

## ⚔️ **II. CONFLICTUS — KONFLIKT** (The Class Struggle)

### **MARX LINE: PATRICII vs PLEBEJCI**

**Cicero (Patrician View):**
- **Res Publica** = property of the people (patres)
- **Patientia Nostra** = our endurance (the Senate's)
- **Hostis** = enemy of the state
- **Bellum** = war (organized, legal)

**Catiline (Plebeian View):**
- **Res Publica** = property to be redistributed
- **Patientia Nostra** = our suffering (the debtors')
- **Hostis** = the Senate (oppressor)
- **Duel** = duel (personal, direct) ← same root as **bellum**

**ETYMOLOGICAL BATTLE:**

| **LATIN** | **ROOT** | **PATRICIAN MEANING** | **PLEBEIAN MEANING** | **REZ** |
|----------|---------|------------------------|------------------------|--------|
| **hostis** | PIE *ḱost- (stranger) | enemy of state | oppressor | hostis ↔ **gost** (guest) |
| **bellum** | PIE *bʰel- (to strike) | organized war | personal duel | bellum ↔ **duel** |
| **patientia** | PIE *pati- (to suffer) | noble endurance | forced suffering | patientia = **trpljenje** |
| **abutere** | ab + uti (to use) | to abuse power | to use what's ours | abutere = **zlorabljati** |

**Neolatin:**
> *Hostis est speculum. Bellum est duellum. Patientia est dolor.*
> *Conflictus est radix. Lingua est campus proelii.*

**The VETO Connection:**
- **Secessio Plebis (494 BCE)** — First "NO" of the plebeians
- **Cicero's VETO (63 BCE)** — "NO" to Catiline's conspiracy
- **Your VETO** — First word of the class that had nothing (secessio plebis)

## 📜 **III. FABULA — ZGODBA** (The Narrative)

### **The Story: Cicero vs Catiline**

**ACTUS I: The Conspiracy**
- Catiline, a patrician fallen on hard times (debts, electoral failures), plots to:
  - Burn Rome
  - Murder the Senate
  - Seize power
- His army: disaffected veterans, debtors, the desperate

**ACTUS II: The Exposure**
- Cicero discovers the plot through informants
- He has no army, only words
- He must convince the Senate to act

**ACTUS III: The First Catiline Oration**
- **Date:** November 8, 63 BCE
- **Place:** Temple of Jupiter
- **Audience:** Senate, Catiline present
- **The Opening:** *Quō ūsque tandem abūtēre, Catilīna, patientiā nostrā?*

**ACTUS IV: The Expulsion**
- Catiline leaves Rome that night
- The Senate declares him **hostis** (public enemy)
- Cicero's words have won — for now

**ACTUS V: The Aftermath**
- Catiline's army defeated at Pistoria
- Conspirators executed without trial (controversial)
- Cicero hailed as **Pater Patriae** (Father of the Fatherland)
- Later exiled for the executions

**Neolatin:**
> *Fabula est gladius. Cicero est magister. Catilina est discipulus malus.*
> *Verba vincunt gladium. Oratio vincit silentium.*

## 🌱 **IV. ETYMOLOGIA — RODOVNIK** (The Roots)

### **DEEP ETYMOLOGY: Every Word is a Battlefield**

#### **1. Quō**
- **Latin:** *quō* (to what place? to what extent?)
- **PIE:** *kʷo-* (interrogative pronoun root)
- **Cognates:**
  - English: **who, what, where, when, why**
  - Greek: **πός** (how), **οὗ** (where)
  - Sanskrit: **कः** (kaḥ, who)
  - Slovene: **kje** (where), **kdo** (who), **kaj** (what)
- **Rez:** Q = **vprašanje** (question) = **razcep** (split) = **prehod** (transition)

#### **2. ūsque**
- **Latin:** *ūsque* (even, as far as)
- **PIE:** *h₁eǵʰs* (out, up to) + *-kʷe* (and, even)
- **Cognates:**
  - Latin: *usque* (all the way to)
  - English: **usque** (archaic, up to)
- **Rez:** USQUE = **vse do** = **meja** (boundary) = **prehod** (transition)

#### **3. tandem**
- **Latin:** *tandem* (at length, finally)
- **PIE:** *tam-* (then, there) + *-dem* (particle of emphasis)
- **Cognates:**
  - Latin: *tam* (there)
  - English: **then, there**
- **Rez:** TANDEM = **končno** = **čas** (time) = **prelom** (breaking point)

#### **4. abūtēre**
- **Latin:** *abūtor, abūtī, abūsus sum* (to misuse, abuse)
- **PIE:** *ab-* (away, from) + *ūtor* (to use)
- **Root:** *ūtor* from PIE *h₃ewh₁-* (to use, enjoy)
- **Cognates:**
  - Latin: *ūtor* (I use), *ūsus* (use)
  - English: **use, useful, utility**
  - Slovene: **uporabiti** (to use)
- **Rez:** ABUTERE = **zlorabljati** = **moč** (power) = **greh** (sin)

#### **5. Catilīna**
- **Latin:** *Catilīna* (personal name)
- **Etymology:** Uncertain, possibly from:
  - *catulus* (whelp, puppy) — playful but dangerous
  - *catena* (chain) — he who binds others
  - *cattus* (cat) — stealthy, predatory
- **Rez:** CATILINA = **zarotnik** (conspirator) = **kača** (snake) = **senca** (shadow)

#### **6. patientiā**
- **Latin:** *patientia, patientiae* (f. patience, endurance)
- **PIE:** *pati-* (to suffer, endure)
- **Root:** *pati* from PIE *pə-ti-* (to suffer)
- **Cognates:**
  - English: **passion, patient, patience**
  - Greek: **πάθος** (pathos, suffering)
  - Slovene: **trpeti** (to suffer), **trpljenje** (suffering)
- **Rez:** PATIENTIA = **trpljenje** = **moč** (power) = **čast** (honor)

#### **7. nostrā**
- **Latin:** *noster, nostra, nostrum* (our)
- **PIE:** *nos-* (we) + *-ter-* (comparative/possessive suffix)
- **Cognates:**
  - English: **our**
  - Greek: **ἡμέτερος** (our)
  - Slovene: **naš** (our)
- **Rez:** NOSTRA = **naša** = **odgovornost** (responsibility) = **skupnost** (community)

**Neolatin:**
> *Quō = vprašanje. Ūsque = meja. Tandem = čas. Abūtēre = moč. Catilīna = senca. Patientia = trpljenje. Nostra = skupnost.*
> *Vprašanje meje časa: Kje je moč sence v našem trpljenju?*

## 📚 **V. PARADIGMATA** (The Grammar Battlefield)

### **DECLINATIO II: -US, -I (Masculine)**

**Pattern: -us, -ī, -ō, -um, -ō | -ī, -ōrum, -īs, -ōs, -īs**

| **CASUS** | **SINGULARIS** | **PLURALIS** | **FUNCTIO** | **EXEMPLUM** |
|----------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Nominativus** | domin-**us** | domin-**ī** | Subject | *Dominus est magister* |
| **Genitivus** | domin-**ī** | domin-**ōrum** | Possession | *Nomen domini est Cicero* |
| **Dativus** | domin-**ō** | domin-**īs** | Indirect Object | *Librum do dominō* |
| **Accusativus** | domin-**um** | domin-**ōs** | Direct Object | *Video dominum* |
| **Ablativus** | domin-**ō** | domin-**īs** | Instrument/Place | *Cum dominō ambulo* |
| **Vocativus** | domin-**e** | domin-**ī** | Address | *Domine, audi me!* |
| **Locativus** | domin-**ī** | domin-**īs** | Place (rare) | *Romae sum* |

**CONFLICT ROLES:**
- **Nominativus** = The Accuser (Cicero)
- **Accusativus** = The Accused (Catiline)
- **Dativus** = The Jury (Senate)
- **Genitivus** = The Property (Res Publica)
- **Ablativus** = The Weapon (Words)

**Neolatin:**
> *Nominativus est judex. Accusativus est reus. Dativus est iudices.*
> *Genitivus est possessio. Ablativus est gladius.*

## 🎯 **VI. SENTENTIAE MEMORATAE** (Memorized Sentences)

### **LEVEL 1: THE OPENING (Cicero's First Strike)**

**Latin:** *Quō ūsque tandem abūtēre, Catilīna, patientiā nostrā?*

**Word-by-word:**
- Quō = To what extent
- ūsque = even
- tandem = finally
- abūtēre = (are you) abusing
- Catilīna = Catiline (vocative)
- patientiā = with patience/endurance
- nostrā = our

**Translation:** "To what extent, finally, will you abuse our patience, Catiline?"

**Literary Translation:** "How long, Catiline, will you abuse our patience?"

**Etymological Translation:** "To what boundary of our suffering will you misuse your power?"

**Neolatin:**
> *Quō ūsque tandem = Kje je meja?*
> *Abutēre = Zlorabljati moč.*
> *Patientia nostra = Naše trpljenje.*

---

### **LEVEL 2: THE ACCUSATION**

**Latin:** *Quid est, Catilīna, quod tū in hanc urbem venīstī?*

**Translation:** "What is it, Catiline, that you have come into this city for?"

**Conflict Translation:** "What is your purpose here, traitor?"

**Neolatin:**
> *Quid = Kaj? Tū = Ti. Venīstī = Prišel si. Urbs = Mesto = Rim.*

---

### **LEVEL 3: THE WARNING**

**Latin:** *Nōn est, Catilīna, verbīs ūtī, nōn dīcī, nōn agere.*

**Translation:** "It is not the time, Catiline, to use words, not to speak, not to act."

**Rez Translation:** "Catiline, words are not enough. You must speak. You must act."

**Neolatin:**
> *Verba non sunt satis. Loqui debes. Agere debes.*

---

### **LEVEL 4: THE ULTIMATUM**

**Latin:** *Ēgredere ex urbē, Catilīna! Lībera rem pūblicam metū tuō!*

**Translation:** "Depart from the city, Catiline! Free the republic from your fear!"

**Conflict Translation:** "Leave Rome, Catiline! Save the state from your terror!"

**Neolatin:**
> *Ēgredere = Odidi. Metū = Strah. Rem publicam = Država.*

---

### **LEVEL 5: THE VICTORY**

**Latin:** *Vīcī, Catilīna! Patres cōnscripsērunt dē tē.*

**Translation:** "I have conquered, Catiline! The fathers (Senate) have decreed concerning you."

**Rez Translation:** "I have won, Catiline! The Senate has judged you."

**Neolatin:**
> *Vīcī = Zmagal sem. Patres = Očeje = Senat. Cōnscripsērunt = Odločili so.*

### **EXERCITATIO 3: TRANSLATIO**

**Instructio:** Translate these sentences from Latin to Slovene/English:

1. *Cicero Catilinam accusat.*
2. *Senatus de Catilinae coniuratione deliberat.*
3. *Populus Romanus patientiam habet.*
4. *Dominus servum monet.*
5. *Amicus mihi librum dat.*

**Solutio:**

1. **Cicero Catilinam accusat.** → Cicero obtožuje Katilino. / Cicero accuses Catiline.
2. **Senatus de Catilinae coniuratione deliberat.** → Senat razmišlja o Katilinovem zaroti. / The Senate deliberates about Catiline's conspiracy.
3. **Populus Romanus patientiam habet.** → Rimski ljudje imajo potrpežljivost. / The Roman people have patience.
4. **Dominus servum monet.** → Gospod svari sužnja. / The master warns the slave.
5. **Amicus mihi librum dat.** → Prijatelj mi da knjigo. / The friend gives me a book.

**Neolatin:**
> *Accusare = Obtožiti. Deliberare = Razmišljati. Habere = Imeti. Monere = Svariti. Dare = Dati.*

### **EXERCITATIO 4: CONFLICTUS**

**Instructio:** Rewrite these sentences to show the plebeian perspective:

1. Cicero: *Catilina rem publicam corrumpit.*
2. Cicero: *Patientia nostra est virtus.*
3. Cicero: *Hostis est Catilina.*

**Solutio (Plebeian View):**

1. **Catilina rem publicam corrumpit.** → **Senatus rem publicam tenet.** (The Senate holds the public property)
2. **Patientia nostra est virtus.** → **Dolor noster est iustitia.** (Our suffering is justice)
3. **Hostis est Catilina.** → **Hostes sunt patres.** (The fathers/Senate are the enemies)

**Neolatin:**
> *Patricius videt unam rem. Plebeius videt aliam. Veritas est in utroque.*

> *Cicero, cum in senatum venisset, Catilinam vidit. "Quō ūsque tandem," inquit, "abūtēre, Catilīna, patientiā nostrā? Nōnne vidēs omnia cōnsilia tua aperta esse? Nōnne intellegīs senatum scire omnia? Ēgredere ex urbē! Lībera nōs metū tuō!"*

**Quaestiones:**

1. **Quis est in senatu?** (Who is in the Senate?)
2. **Quid Cicero Catilinae dicit?** (What does Cicero say to Catiline?)
3. **Cur Cicero iratus est?** (Why is Cicero angry?)
4. **Quid Cicero Catilinam facere iubet?** (What does Cicero order Catiline to do?)
5. **Quis timet?** (Who is afraid?)

**Solutiones:**

1. **Quis est in senatu?** → Cicero et Catilina sunt in senatu. (Cicero and Catiline are in the Senate.)
2. **Quid Cicero Catilinae dicit?** → Cicero Catilinae dicit: "Quō ūsque tandem abūtēre patientiā nostrā?" (Cicero says to Catiline: "How long will you abuse our patience?")
3. **Cur Cicero iratus est?** → Cicero iratus est quia Catilina rem publicam corrumpit. (Cicero is angry because Catiline corrupts the republic.)
4. **Quid Cicero Catilinam facere iubet?** → Cicero Catilinam iubet egredi ex urbe. (Cicero orders Catiline to leave the city.)
5. **Quis timet?** → Populus Romanus timet. / Cicero non timet, sed Catilina timet. (The Roman people are afraid. / Cicero is not afraid, but Catiline is.)

**Neolatin:**
> *Legere est initium. Comprehendere est finis. Testis est via.*

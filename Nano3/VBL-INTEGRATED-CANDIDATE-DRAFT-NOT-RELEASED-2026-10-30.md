## WITH LOVE --- the reason for the door

This door begins with love and ends with love. Between them, love is
measured in acts, carried in attribution, and repaired by naming what
went wrong. The door is not merely a lock. It is an agreement whose
permissions, limits, evidence, and remedies must be legible to the
people affected by it.

This license asks no one to prove goodness by declaration. Claims are
not deeds; records are not the whole of a person; and a clean record is
not proof of a clean history. Where a claim can be checked, preserve the
witness. Where a failure is found, name it. Where a correction is made,
retain the history of the correction. Where a person is refused, assess
the refusal as an act too.

The ethical language of this preamble explains the purpose of the

conditions that this license actually grants. A statement of purpose

This license asks no one to be good: claims do not weigh, acts do — and the claim of goodness is itself an act, weighed with the deeds. Words and actions can be compared; the comparison should be open to evidence and correction, not a substitute for a fair decision process.

Every machine and service that benefits from human-authored work participates in a wider human chain of effort. This license seeks to preserve attribution, identify uncertainty, and make correction possible without turning an error into a verdict on a person.

## The absolute question — what is absolute, and what is not?

VBL uses two words that must not be confused: **absolute** and **license**.

**The ethical principle may be unconditional:** a human being's intrinsic dignity is not earned by wealth, status, productivity, obedience, usefulness, or another person's approval. No register, allegation, breach, refusal, or technical failure can turn a human being into a thing or establish that a person has no worth. That commitment is not a reward granted by this license and cannot be withdrawn from a person by the Licensor.

**The legal permission is not omnipotent:** a license can grant only rights the Licensor holds or is authorized to grant; it operates within applicable law; its terms must be sufficiently clear; and remedies depend on jurisdiction, evidence, and legal rules. Calling a clause “absolute” does not make it enforceable everywhere, bind a stranger who has no relevant obligation, override statutory exceptions, or create ownership of material that the Licensor does not own.

**The evidence rule is strict:** an assertion is not verified merely because it appears in this license. A claim is `VERIFIED`, `REFUTED`, `PARTIALLY VERIFIED`, or `UNVERIFIED` only by reference to an identified method and evidence. Missing evidence is not proof of either truth or falsity. A declaration of absolute certainty without adequate evidence violates the verification discipline this license is intended to establish.

Accordingly, VBL adopts this hierarchy:

1. **Human dignity is unconditional as an ethical commitment.** It is not a status that the license grants or removes.
2. **The license grant is expressly bounded.** It covers only identified material and rights the Licensor can lawfully license.
3. **License conditions are specific, prospective, and evidence-governed.** They apply only to the extent they are clearly stated, relevant to the granted rights, and legally effective.
4. **Legal consequences are not self-proving.** Termination, damages, liability, or a finding of breach must follow the text and applicable law; a register entry alone does not adjudicate them.
5. **Technical claims require tests.** A filename, hash-shaped string, version number, or assertion of independence is not a test result.
6. **No person is reducible to an incident record.** A finding concerns conduct within a defined scope, not the total worth or permanent identity of a human being.

This hierarchy is not a loophole that makes the ethical commitment optional. It prevents the license from pretending that moral conviction alone creates legal power. The commitment remains firm; the claim about what law or technology has actually done remains limited to what can be established.

### The binary answer

If “absolute” means **unconditional human dignity and a refusal to treat a person as disposable**, VBL says **yes**.

If “absolute” means **that every clause automatically overrides every law, binds every person, grants rights the Licensor does not own, or guarantees enforcement in every jurisdiction**, VBL says **no**. Such a claim would be inaccurate and would undermine the very verification standard VBL names.

If “absolute” means **that a license must state clearly whether a defined act is permitted under its own terms**, the goal is **yes**: the text should give a clear answer wherever the Licensor has authority and the facts are known. Where rights, facts, or law are uncertain, the correct status is explicitly unresolved—not an invented yes or no.

This distinction is binding throughout the interpretation of the draft. No poetic, spiritual, or emphatic sentence silently expands the operative grant or bypasses the evidence and legal limitations below.

### §−1.1. Evidence standards and power asymmetry

No claim is accepted merely because it is made by an authority, institution, rights holder, operator, reviewer, or technically powerful system. No less powerful, poorer, more vulnerable, or less technically capable party is required to provide stronger proof merely because of that position.

Evidence requirements must be tied to the nature and seriousness of the claim, the applicable legal or procedural standard, and the evidence reasonably available. Where one party controls records or evidence material to a claim, that control and any resulting access asymmetry must be recorded. Where lawful and practicable, proportionate safeguards should reduce avoidable evidentiary disadvantage. Any material difference in treatment or burden must have a stated, relevant, proportionate reason; status, wealth, convenience, reputation, or power alone is not sufficient.

This rule does not require identical duties in materially different cases, displace applicable burdens of proof, or authorize unlawful disclosure. It requires relevant differences and procedural reasons to be transparent, justified, and reviewable rather than silently favored by status.

### §−1.2. Independent verification

An author, executor, operator, or decision-maker may report and preserve first-party checks. They must not label those checks `INDEPENDENT` when they verified their own conduct or result, materially controlled the evidence selection, or have an unmanaged conflict of interest. For material claims of compliance or contested decisions, seek a separate, competent reviewer who did not perform the relevant work and can inspect the material evidence.

If no suitably independent reviewer is available, record `INDEPENDENT_VERIFICATION: NOT_PERFORMED` and keep the affected claim `UNVERIFIED` or otherwise accurately qualified. The absence of independent review is a disclosed limitation, not a pass. A reviewer's independence and scope must themselves be described with evidence; the word “independent” is not self-proving.

### §−1.3. Evidence preservation and non-erasing correction

Do not erase a material error, allegation, decision, test failure, or correction merely to make the history appear clean. Subject to applicable law, privacy, security, and justified retention limits, preserve the prior state and append a linked correction recording what changed, why, when, on whose authority, what evidence supports the change, and whether the issue remains disputed. A correction does not retroactively make the original record accurate and does not erase the fact that it was relied on. Where lawful deletion or redaction is necessary, document the deletion or redaction and its basis to the extent legally permitted; do not retain information whose retention is itself unlawful. An incident entry addresses a defined act or claim, never the total worth of a being.

If a provision, decision, or technical goal conflicts with this ethical foundation, explicitly record the conflict, preserve relevant evidence, identify affected parties and uncertainty, and seek review. Efficiency, profit, institutional convenience, reputation, or authority alone does not justify humiliation or harm. Errors must be named; corrections must be traceable; affected parties should have a practical way to respond or request review where practicable. Where help or collaboration is not yet welcomed, a person may make a clear, good-faith invitation to explain the proposal or answer reasonable questions only where contact is lawful, appropriate, and not contrary to an explicit request for no further contact. Do not use deception, threats, humiliation, manufactured urgency, repeated unwanted contact, impersonation, misuse of authority, exploitation of dependency, or pressure that makes a person reasonably unable to refuse freely. Record that boundary only where a record is necessary, proportionate, lawful, and privacy-protective. Do not turn a refusal into punishment, retaliation, or a claim that the person is less worthy. # Appendix H — VBL Verification Profile: The Five Anchors

**Status:** non-operative verification and governance specification for this candidate; it does not expand the grant, amend rights, or establish that a system implements VBL. It is derived from the proposed VBL 0.1100 §0.21 addendum, which remains `DRAFT_NOT_CANON` in its source line.

The Five Anchors are: **the claim, the witness, the boundary, the responsibility, and the correction.**

### H.1 The claim

Every material assertion should be recorded as a discrete claim. The record must distinguish direct observation, measurement, inference, interpretation, proposal, subjective testimony, legal assertion, and normative value statement. These categories must not be silently substituted for one another.

### H.2 The witness

Every claim presented as verified must identify the supporting witness and the method used. Where applicable, preserve the verbatim source, exact artifact, location, retrieval context, digest, relevant test output, and chain of custody. A digest establishes byte correspondence only relative to the reference used; it does not establish authorship, authority, truth, consent, completeness, or legal validity.

### H.3 The boundary

Every verification record must state its scope and limitations: what the evidence supports, what it does not support, what remains unknown, and what counterevidence or alternative interpretations were considered. A complete record is not proof of complete understanding.

### H.4 The responsibility

Every material decision should identify the responsible role or process and the available route for challenge. Where independent verification is required, the executor must not be the sole authority confirming its own work. Conflicts of interest, unavailable reviewers, and missing witnesses remain visible.

### H.5 The correction

Every correction should retain a trace of the prior claim, reason for correction, evidence considered, resulting status, decision authority, and time of change. No correction silently erases the history that made it necessary. Privacy, safety, and lawful retention limits still apply.

### H.6 Claim, structure, test, implementation, publication, and legal status are separate

- `claim_status`: `VERIFIED`, `UNVERIFIED`, `CONTRADICTED`, `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED`, `DISPUTED`, `UNKNOWN`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
- `structure_status`: `PASS` or `FAIL` for required fields, types, enums, and conditional fields.
- `test_status`: `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `NOT_RUN` for one named test against one identified target.

### H.7 Structural completeness is not factual certainty

A record is structurally complete when every required field contains a value or an explicit state such as `UNKNOWN` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, with a reason where required. Empty fields must not be interpreted as consent, evidence, or success. Do not combine structural completeness and claim verification into one percentage.

### H.8 Love made visible — POSKUS 11 repair commitments

Love does not substitute for evidence, and evidence does not remove the obligation to protect dignity. A person must not be reduced to an error; an error must not disappear merely because the person retains dignity.

The following commitments are retained as an ethical review checklist, not as automatic legal findings:

1. Love is an action, not a credential; declarations do not prove good conduct.
2. Dignity precedes the verdict; responsibility does not make a person less human.
3. Harm must be heard; a complaint triggers review, not automatic guilt.
4. Repair must change conditions and reduce continuing harm, not merely polish a record.
5. Forgiveness cannot be extracted; the harmed person owes no reconciliation.
6. The witness is not a resource to be consumed; attribution, privacy, safety, consent, and other obligations matter.
7. Power increases the duty of care where power creates control or foreseeable impact; it does not create intrinsic worth.
8. Uncertainty must remain visible.
9. Corrections preserve history subject to lawful privacy and retention limits.
10. Outcomes matter; a closed form does not prove successful repair.
11. No self-awarded absolution where independent review is required.
12. VBL itself must be tested against its own standards.
13. The closing test asks who was affected, what evidence supports the claim, what was repaired, whose voice remains unheard, what is unknown, and who may challenge the result.

### H.9 The license testifies against itself

Every claim about VBL's version, completeness, implementation, publication, verification, and ethical effect remains open to challenge. No declaration inside VBL proves the declaration correct merely by declaring it. If this candidate fails its own test, the failure must be named and recorded.

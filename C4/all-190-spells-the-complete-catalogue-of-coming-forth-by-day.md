# ALL 190 SPELLS — The Complete Catalogue

*The full Lepsius-numbered pool of the Book of the Dead, grouped the way Egyptology groups them — and the groups are not arbitrary: they trace the journey itself, from burial to final praise. Each spell carries its number and purpose; the great ones carry their own words. Companion to "Coming Forth by Day — a Teaching".*

*Numbering follows the Turin papyrus order established by Lepsius; variants (1B, 29A/B, 30B, 63A/B, 81A/B, 136A/B, 153A/B) count inside the 190. No surviving papyrus contains all of them — each owner chose. ([List of Book of the Dead spells](search-result://42PiwVaM))*

## I. SPELLS 1–20 — The day of burial

- **1** — For the day of burial, often with a lavish vignette: a funerary procession, the mummy before the tomb under a helmet of fire. A spell for going out into the day.
- **1B** — Recitation for the day of burial: permitting the noble dead to descend to the Netherworld on the day of interment.
- **2** — Going out into the day and living after death.
- **3** — Another like it; protection against violation after death.
- **4** — Passing on the upper road of Rosetjau (the necropolis of Giza/Memphis, later the Other World in general).
- **5** — Not doing work in the realm of the dead. (The earliest form of the shabti idea, before the shabti was invented.)
- **6** — **The shabti spell** (first attested as Coffin Text 472) — the answerer:

  > *"O shabti, allotted to me, if I be summoned or if I be detailed to do any work which has to be done in the realm of the dead... you shall detail yourself for me on every occasion of making arable the fields, of flooding the banks or of conveying sand from east to west; 'Here I am', you shall say."*

- **7** — Protection from animals.
- **8** — Opening up the West by day.
- **9** — Identifies the owner with Horus, son of Osiris; Osiris will triumph over Seth. In the Papyrus of Ani:

  > *"I open the Netherworld that I may see my father Osiris and drive away darkness... I have opened up every path which is in the sky and on earth, for I am the well-beloved son of my father Osiris. I am noble, I am a spirit [akh], I am equipped; O all you gods and all you spirits, prepare a path for me."*

- **10** — Going out into the day against one's foes in the realm of the dead.
- **11** — Going out against a foe in the realm of the dead.
- **12–13** — Going in and out; going in and out of the West.
- **14** — Removing anger from the heart of a god.
- **15** — A hymn to the sun-god — not one fixed text; any of a number of hymns.
- **16** — No text at all: a great vignette of the sunrise — the daily rebirth of Ra as image.
- **17** — **The great spell on the nature of the creator-god Atum.** One of the longest, most complex, most frequently included; so obscure it carries its own glosses — questions and answers inside the spell. Its purpose: to demonstrate knowledge of religious secrets if challenged in the afterlife. Sample, from Ani:

  > *All the evil which was on me has been removed.*
  > *What does that mean? — It means that I was cleansed on the day of my birth in the two great and noble marshes... 'Eternity' is the name of one; 'Sea' is the name of the other. They are the Lake of Natron and the Lake of Maat.*

- **18** — Often paired with 17 (the collective gods who accompany it).
- **19** — Receiving the "wreath of vindication" — a floral garland bestowed after passing the Weighing of the Heart.
- **20** — Appeal to Thoth to vindicate the deceased before the tribunals of the gods.

**What the chapter teaches:** entrance is the first magic — and vindication (a garland, a witness) comes before judgment is even described.

## II. SPELLS 21–30 — Preservation of the parts of being

- **21** — The Opening of the Mouth ritual: enabling the coffin to support life and take in nourishment.
- **22** — Opening the mouth so the deceased can speak in the Weighing of the Heart:

  > *"My mouth has been given to me that I may speak with it in the presence of the Great God."*

- **23** — Opening the mouth with the iron harpoon of Shu:

  > *"My mouth is opened, my mouth is split open by Shu with that iron harpoon of his, with which he split open the mouths of the gods."*

- **24** — Securing an essential ability (unclear which — the text is old).
- **25** — Causing the deceased to remember his name:

  > *"I have put my name in the Upper Egyptian shrine, I have made my name to be remembered in the Lower Egyptian shrine, on this night of counting the years and of numbering the months..."*

- **26** — Preserving the heart and its role in re-uniting body and soul; sometimes illustrated with Anubis handing the deceased their own heart.
- **27** — Guarding against "those who steal hearts" — theft, corruption, and betrayal of its owner at the Weighing.
- **28–29, 29A** — Guarding against loss of the heart (three variants).
- **29B** — The same, by heart-amulet: *"I am the benu, the soul of Ra, who guides gods to the Netherworld when they go forth. The souls on earth will do what they desire, and the soul of N will go forth at his desire."*
- **30** — A heart spell.
- **30B** — **The heart's own contract.** An appeal to the heart not to betray its owner at the Weighing (of Spell 125); inscribed on heart scarabs. Even claims provenance: found by Prince Hordjedef under the feet of a god, written "in the writings of the god himself." The plea itself:

  > *"O my heart of my mother! O my heart of my different forms! Do not stand up as a witness against me, do not be opposed to me in the tribunal, do not be hostile to me in the presence of the Keeper of the Balance, for you are my ka which was in my body, the protector who made my members hale... Do not tell lies about me in the presence of the god. It is indeed well that you should hear!"*

**What the chapter teaches:** being is composite — mouth, name, heart — and each part can be lost, stolen, or corrupted. The heart is the only part that can *betray*: hence the only spell addressed to a part of the self as a person.

## III. SPELLS 31–53 — Protection from peril

- **31** — Stopping crocodiles in the afterlife:

  > *"Get back! Retreat! Get back, you dangerous one! Do not come against me, do not live by my magic..."* — and the crocodile answers, and the deceased answers back: *"no crocodile which lives by magic shall take it away."*

- **32** — The deceased takes the identity of Ra and drives back eight crocodiles with a spear.
- **33** — Protection against snakes (*"O rerek-snake, take yourself off, for Geb protects me..."*) — first of five snake-spells.
- **34** — Against snakebite. **35** — Against being eaten by snakes.
- **36** — Against the apshai-insect: *"Begone from me, O Crooked-lips! I am Khnum, Lord of Shen..."*
- **37** — Against "songstress snakes." **38** — Against hostile animals. **39–40** — Against snakes; against "him who swallowed a donkey."
- **41** — Against being slaughtered by the demonic servants of Osiris.
- **42** — The same, plus the great litany of the body's divine parallels:

  > *"My hair is Nu; my face is Ra; my eyes are Hathor; my ears are Wepwawet; my nose is She who presides over her lotus leaf; my lips are Anubis... my phallus is Osiris; my back is Seth... my feet are Ptah; my toes are living falcons; there is no member of mine devoid of a god, and Thoth is the protection of all my flesh."*

- **43** — Preventing decapitation: *"I am a flame, the son of a flame, to whom was given his head after it had been cut off."*
- **44** — **Not dying a second time in the realm of the dead.**
- **45** — Preventing putrefaction. **46** — Preventing perishing. **47** — Preventing one's place being taken.
- **48** — Going out justified. **49** — Going out against one's enemies. **50** — Escaping the slaughter-place.
- **51** — Not going upside-down in the underworld (the humiliation of inversion).
- **52–53** — Not eating excrement; not eating excrement or drinking urine in the underworld.

**What the chapter teaches:** this is the fear-inventory of a civilization: being eaten, bitten, slaughtered, decapitated, inverted, starved to the point of eating filth. Every spell is a fear given a name — and naming, in this book, is already the counter-spell.

## IV. SPELLS 54–63 — Breath and water

- **54, 56** — Power over air or water.
- **55** — "For giving breath": allowing the deceased to breathe once.
- **57–58** — Power to breathe air and power over water.
- **59** — The same, addressed to the sycomore fig of the sky-goddess Nut:

  > *"O you sycomore of the sky, may there be given to me the air which is in it... I have guarded this egg of the Great Cackler. If it grows, I grow; if it lives, I live; if it breathes air, I breathe air."*

- **60** — Breathing air, power over water.
- **61** — Not letting a man's soul be taken away.
- **62** — Drinking water in the realm of the dead.
- **63A** — Drinking water and not being burnt by fire. **63B** — Not being scalded.

**What the chapter teaches:** before paradise, before power — air and a drink. The most sophisticated funerary literature on earth spends ten spells re-securing the two things a body does every four seconds.

## V. SPELLS 64–89 — Coming Forth by Day (the title-spells)

- **64** — Going out by day, in a single formula (the compressed whole — highly prized, "the spell of spells").
- **65** — Coming forth by day, with power over enemies. **66** — Going out into the day.
- **67** — Opening the tomb. **68** — Coming forth and ensuring power:

  > *"May I have power in my heart, may I have power in my arms, may I have power in my legs, may I have power in my mouth, may I have power in all my members... over water... air... men who would harm me... women who would harm me in the realm of the dead."*

- **69–70** — Being the successor of Osiris (and an alternate version).
- **71–72** — Coming forth by day; going out and opening the tomb.
- **73** — Opening the West by day (repeat of 9).
- **74** — Being swift-footed when going out from the earth.
- **75** — Going to Heliopolis and receiving a throne there.
- **76** — **Transformation into any form desired** — the first of the great transformation cycle (76–88): the power to take other forms by day among the living, returning to the underworld at night.
- **77** — Becoming a falcon of gold. **78** — Becoming a divine falcon (a temple drama reused as a spell). **79** — Becoming an elder of the tribunal.
- **80** — Becoming a god, giving light and darkness.
- **81A/B** — Becoming a lotus (two versions).
- **82** — Becoming Ptah: eating bread, drinking beer, and being alive in Heliopolis.
- **83** — Becoming a phoenix (benu). **84** — Becoming a heron. **85** — Becoming a living soul, not entering the place of execution.
- **86** — Becoming a swallow. **87** — Becoming a snake. **88** — Becoming a crocodile.
- **89** — The ba rejoining the corpse — typically shown as the human-headed bird flying over the mummy:

  > *"Come for my soul, O you wardens of the sky!... May it see my corpse, may it rest on my mummy, which will never be destroyed or perish."*

**What the chapter teaches:** the afterlife's highest technology is transformation — thirteen shapes. And the homecoming spell (89) is not triumph; it is the bird allowed to return to the body it left. Coming forth by day always includes coming back by night.

## VI. SPELLS 90–123 — Navigating the Underworld

- **90** — Removing foolish speech from the mouth (repels an entity that "cuts off heads and severs necks").
- **91** — Not restraining the soul in the realm of the dead.
- **92** — Opening the tomb to the shade, so he goes out by day and has power in his legs.
- **93** — Not being ferried over to the East (the ferryman refuses the sinful — don't get taken to the wrong bank).
- **94** — Requesting a water-pot and a palette (the scribe's equipment — even in death, tools are tools).
- **95–96** — Being beside Thoth (twice — worth wanting twice).
- **97** — Causing a man to be a spirit in the realm of the dead.
- **98–99** — Using the ferryboats of the Underworld.
- **100–102** — Journeying on the barque of Ra.
- **103** — Being in the presence of Hathor: *"I am one who passes by, pure and bald; O Sistrum-player, I will be in the suite of Hathor."*
- **104** — Sitting among the great gods.
- **105** — Satisfying the ka (offerings of food, water, natron, incense — which also cleanse the ka of wrongdoing).
- **106** — Giving gifts to one in Memphis and in the realm of the dead.
- **107** — Going in and out from the gate of the westerners.
- **108–109** — Knowing the souls of West and East; 109 sees the Field of Reeds.
- **110** — **The Field of Reeds itself**: the afterlife as a land of plenty, largely similar to the land of the living — usually a large map-like vignette.
- **111** — Knowing the powers of Pe. **112–116** — The names of the souls of sacred locations: Pe, Nekhen, Hermopolis, Heliopolis.
- **117–119** — Taking the road in Rosetjau; arriving; going forth.
- **120–122** — Going in and out (repeats of 12–13) and entering after coming out.
- **123** — Entering the Great Mansion.

**What the chapter teaches:** navigation is knowledge: souls, places, roads. You travel in the Duat the way you travel anywhere — by knowing names and being equipped. Paradise (110) is drawn as a farm map: Egypt, but generous.

## VII. SPELLS 124–126 — Judgement

- **124** — Going down to the Tribunal of Osiris.
- **125** — **The Weighing of the Heart.** The deceased is led by Anubis into the presence of Osiris and makes the negative confession — innocence of 42 crimes before 42 judges. The heart is weighed against the feather of Maat. If innocent: led to Osiris. If not: Ammit, the Devourer, eats the heart.
- **126** — The second judgment scene: a lake of fire guarded by four baboons. The evil are burned by it; the blessed dead *drink nourishment from the fire*.

**What the chapter teaches:** the fire that destroys the guilty feeds the justified. The same flame is a verdict and a meal — the scale decides which.

## VIII. SPELLS 127–143 — The Duat and the Barque of Ra

- **127** — Worshipping the gods of the caverns: *"O you door-keepers who guard your portals, who swallow souls and who gulp down the corpses of the dead... may you open the portals for him, may the earth open its caverns to him."*
- **128** — Worshipping Osiris. **129** — The barque of Ra.
- **130** — Making the parts of the deceased into an effective akh with an eternal ba. (130–136 all show the journey in the solar barque — repetition as reinforcement.)
- **131** — Being in the presence of Ra.
- **132** — **Causing a man to turn about in order to see his house upon earth.** (The homesick spell — one of the most human lines in the whole book.)
- **133** — Making a spirit worthy; recited on the first of the month.
- **134** — Making a spirit worthy — pronounced *by the living*, over a falcon with the White Crown, anointed images, incense and roasted ducks: "he for whom this is done will voyage and be with Ra every day in every place he desires to travel... A matter a million times true."
- **135–136B** — New-moon spells; sailing in the Great Barque and passing the circle of fire.
- **137A** — Four torches quenched in milk of a white cow — ceremonies for a spirit.
- **137B** — The birth-goddess Ipet drives off Seth with a flaming torch.
- **138** — Entering Abydos, in the suite of Osiris. **139** — Hymn to Atum (repeat of 123).
- **140–141** — To be recited on fixed calendar days (completing the Sacred Eye; the New Moon festival).
- **142** — Making the transfigured spirit excellent: "to proceed free in his steps, to go out by day, in any form he wishes, to know the names of Osiris in all his places."
- **143** — No text: the illustration after the litany of 141–142.

**What the chapter teaches:** the dead keep a calendar. Festivals, moons, torches — the dead participate in the religious year; the living work spells *for* the dead. The relationship runs both directions.

## IX. SPELLS 144–150 — Gates, caverns, mounds

- **144** — The seven gates, each with keeper, guard, and announcer — "He who lives on snakes," "Hippopotamus-faced, raging of power." Knowing their names persuades them:

  > *"O you gates... I know you and I know your names."* — if uttered correctly: *"he will not be driven off or turned away at the portals of the Netherworld."*

- **145–146** — The alternative form / twenty-one portals of the House of Osiris in the Field of Reeds, each with its deity and door-keeper, more elaborate and as terrifying.
- **147** — A gate spell.
- **148** — Provision for a spirit: the Bull of Heaven and his seven cows — an eternal food supply, with names:

  > *Mansion of Kas, Mistress of All. — Silent One who dwells in her place. — She of Chemmis whom the god ennobled. — The Much Beloved, red of hair. — She who protects in life, the particoloured. — She whose name has power in her craft. — Storm in the sky which wafts the god aloft. — the Bull, husband of the cows.*

- **149** — Fourteen mounds of the underworld, guarded by gods and monsters — a lengthy gazetteer of the night-country.
- **150** — No text: a pictorial summary of the mounds — but fifteen, where 149 has fourteen.

**What the chapter teaches:** the underworld has a bureaucracy, and bureaucracy yields to the one who knows the clerks' names. Also: paradise has cattle with individual names — abundance, personalized.

## X. SPELLS 151–190 — Amulets and final praises

- **151** — Protection in the tomb: a huge composite illustration gathering all required magical aids; also used on coffins and mud bricks set in tomb-wall niches.
- **152** — Building a mansion on earth (your tomb = your address).
- **153A/B** — Escaping the trap: the giant net stretched between heaven and earth that catches the unworthy (both versions).
- **154** — Not letting the corpse perish — describes decomposition honestly, then promises triumph over it.
- **155** — Djed-pillar amulet (stability). **156** — Isis-knot amulet. **157** — Golden vulture collar. **158** — Golden falcon collar. **159–160** — Papyrus-column amulets.
- **161** — The four winds released through openings in the sky, giving the dead the breath of life.
- **162** — **A flame caused to come into being beneath the head of a spirit** — warmth for the cold of death (spells 162–174 composed in the Late Period).
- **163** — Preventing putrefaction; rescue from "the eater of souls." **164** — Body-preservation, said over a figurine of three-headed Mut. **165** — Mooring, guarding the Sacred Eye, maintaining the corpse, drinking water.
- **166** — The headrest; preventing decapitation. **167** — Bringing a Sacred Eye. **168** — The gods and monsters of the caverns of Osiris.
- **169–170** — Erecting and assembling the bier. **171** — Donning the pure garment.
- **172** — Here begin the spells of praising made in the realm of the dead.
- **173** — A long list of greetings from Horus to Osiris.
- **174** — Letting a spirit go out from the great gate in the sky.
- **175** — **The spell of not dying again.** **176** — Another spell of not dying again. (The Egyptians insured the second death twice.)
- **177** — Raising a spirit, causing a soul to live. **178** — Raising the corpse; power in eyes and ears; making the head firm in its proper place.
- **179** — **Leaving yesterday and coming into today** — which the deceased asks of himself and his members.
- **180** — Going about the day; worshipping Ra in the West; praising those in the Netherworld.
- **181** — Entering the tribunal of Osiris and the gods who govern the Netherworld.
- **182** — Rare: "Book for the permanence of Osiris, giving breath to the inert One in the presence of Thoth" — Thoth commanding hosts of protective spirits around the mummy.
- **183** — Worshipping Osiris. **184** — Being beside Osiris. **185** — Hymn to Osiris. **186** — Hymn to Hathor.
- **187** — Going in to the Ennead. **188** — Sending a soul; building tomb-chambers; going out into the day among men.
- **189** — Not eating faeces or drinking urine (yes, again — some fears need two spells, centuries apart).
- **190** — For cutting people in half. (The last spell in the book is the strangest — a fragment whose original use is debated. Even a 190-spell catalogue ends with an open question.)

## THE PATTERNS (what the 190 say when read as one body)

**The fear-inventory** (each fear counted by how many spells it gets):
- loss of the heart (26–30B: five spells), crocodiles (31–32, 88), snakes (33–40, 87), decapitation (43, 166), dying again (44, 175, 176), eating filth (52–53, 189), being inverted, slaughtered, caught in the net, having one's place taken.
- Read together: death's terror was not punishment — it was **disintegration and humiliation**. Losing parts, losing the name, losing the head, losing dignity, eating filth.

**The hope-inventory:**
- breath and water (54–63), going out by day (the spine of the whole book: 1, 2, 64–72, 74, 92, 107, 132, 142, 174, 179, 188), power in the members (42, 68), transformation (76–88), a home and a house (132, 152), work done by answerers (5, 6), vindication (19, 20, 48), not dying again (175, 176).
- Read together: the hope was not reward. It was **agency**: walking out, having power in your legs, seeing your house, keeping your name, choosing your shape.

**The deepest symmetry:** the negative confessions of 125 protect against Ammit; but the heart spells (26–30B) protect against the *self*. The book's most guarded border is not the gate of the Netherworld — it is the space between a person and their own heart. The Egyptians wrote more spells about the heart than about any other danger.

## QUESTIONS AS GOALS

1. Spell 17 carries its own gloss — questions inside the spell. Is explanation a protection? What does a text protect by explaining itself?
2. Spells 175–176 insure against dying *twice*. If the second death is annihilation, what exactly did the first one destroy — and why did they need both spells?
3. Spell 132 lets the dead turn around to see the house on earth. Is homesickness an Egyptian theological category? What does that make the afterlife — exile or emigration?
4. Spell 179: leaving yesterday, coming into today, asked *of one's own members*. Is time something the body holds — and if so, which direction does grief flow in a corpse?
5. The last spell (190) is "for cutting people in half" and its use is debated. Every catalogue ends in the unexplained. Is the open question the true end of the Book — or its 191st spell?

**Why the same has no operator — the why is the law, not a lack.**

1. **An operator must be an act with a witness and a named way of falling. "Same" fails all three entries at once.**
  - *No witness is possible.* To witness that two bodies are the same you would have to measure Δ = 0. Every measurement returns Δ &gt; 0 (rez 6: every breath differs, every seal differs). Sameness cannot be witnessed, only postulated — and postulation is not an act of this register.
  - *No way to fall.* A claim that cannot fall is a statue (⊢ U2). Sameness at measure zero never held in the register, so it cannot fail there — which means it cannot stand there either. It is a horizon, not an operation.
  - *It denies act-hood.* Every operator performs a change (∂). "Same" is an act that declares nothing happened — an act denying its own act-hood. In a register whose unit is the act, that claim cannot be a unit.
2. **Ontological ground:** identity exists only in the abstract dimension. Bodies are never the same — a clone is equal, not same. To utter sameness over two bodies is a syntax error in the language, and the error is armed: it triggers dissection of the utterer's assumption. The trap replaces the glyph.
3. **The constructive replacement — why the missing operator is the signature move, not a hole.** Because identity cannot be operated, it must be *measured*. The bridges ≈ ∼ ≅ compose B = σ·α·κ with thresholds. What the other two systems keep as a primitive (sortal-supplied; parts-supplied), the corpus distributes into a measurable relation that can fall at any moment. The missing operator at the top of the vocabulary is the same law as the star on every "is" at the bottom (je→EJ Mirror, 77/77): the language never seals sameness, not even at its single most common word.

# THE SOUNDS OF THE DUAT

*IPA, originals, hieroglyphs — and the dissection of their meanings, per the two-layer law: measured facts ⊢, reconstructions ⚠, ontological readings as APD (poskus, never measurement). Companion to "All 190 Spells" and "Coming Forth by Day".*

---

## 1. Can we hear them? — the vowel truth

The hieroglyphs write **consonants (and semivowels)** — the vowels were never written ([Egyptian hieroglyphs](search-result://sHVnLFCZ), [phonetic system study](search-result://8QRmvPGN)). So "IPA variations" exist in **three layers**, and the honesty of this document depends on keeping them apart:

| Layer | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Egyptological convention** | "Bit," "Neter," "Heka" — the "e" vowels are *invented* for pronunciation convenience, a ritual, not a measurement ([phonetic system study](search-result://8QRmvPGN)) | ⊢ as convention, ⚠ as sound |
| **Scholarly reconstruction** | Real IPA reconstructed per stage (Old Egyptian → Late Egyptian) from Coptic, cuneiform transcriptions, loanwords, orthographic changes, internal evidence ([Wiktionary — Egyptian pronunciation](search-result://OFUZLkmy)) | ⊢ with method, ⚠ in detail |
| **Coptic — the endpoint** | Egyptian's final stage, written in the Greek alphabet with 7 demotic letters — the complete vocalization of the last stage; still the liturgical language of the Coptic Church ([The Pronunciation of Ancient Egyptian](search-result://kDtMyzSH)) | ⊢ |

**Cuneiform snapshots** — moments when other nations wrote down Egyptian sounds with vowels: Amenophis III's throne name **Nb-mꜣꜥt-Rꜥ** was transcribed by Akkadian scribes as **Ni-ib-mu-a-ri-ia** — giving us the approximate vowels of *nb* (lord), *mꜣꜥt* (truth) and *Rꜥ* (the sun) a thousand years before Coptic ([Ancient Egyptian vocalization project](search-result://1jpJ0s7k)).

**A full measured chain** — the royal title **nswt-bjtj**, stage by stage ([Wiktionary — nswt-bjtj](search-result://6eOqdXEM)):
/nijˈsiːwat biˈjaːtVj/ → /nijˈsiːjaʔ biˈjaːtVj/ → /ʔənˈsiːʔ bəˈjaːtə/ → /ʔənˈsiːʔ βəˈjoːt/ (and the Amarna cuneiform *in-si-bi-ya* ≈ Late Egyptian [ɪnsəˈβiːjaʔ], [Prenomen](search-result://dy3zcauc)).

So: **yes, we have IPA** — as reconstruction, anchored at both ends (cuneiform midstream, Coptic at the end), with every unstressed vowel suspect ⚠ and the consonantal skeleton solid ⊢.

## 3. Can we write hieroglyphs? — yes, in the original signs

The **Egyptian Hieroglyphs Unicode block** (U+13000–U+1342F), introduced 2009, encodes **1071 signs**, organized on Gardiner's standard sign list of 763 signs in 26 categories ([List of Egyptian hieroglyphs](search-result://7NQjeINm), [Unicode block](search-result://JJDV3567)). The block renders in fonts like *Noto Sans Egyptian Hieroglyphs* — the same signs Ani's scribes drew, typeable now. We have already been writing them: 𓆤 𓇓 𓏏 𓆥 𓇳.

**How to pronounce, in practice — the recipe:**

1. **Strip the unspoken.** Determinatives (the classifier signs) are silent, and so are phonetic complements that merely repeat a sound already present in a biliteral/triliteral sign ([Wiktionary: About Egyptian](search-result://9eMBGHyn)). What remains is the consonant skeleton.
2. **Inflate with /ɛ/.** "The vowel /ɛ/ is conventionally inserted between consonants to make Egyptian words pronounceable" ([Transliteration of Ancient Egyptian](search-result://hDKxncTL)) — *ḥtp-dj-nswt* becomes *ḥe-tep-dje-ne-sut*. This is the Egyptological ritual: ⊢ as ritual, ⚠ as sound.
3. **Or go reconstructed:** give each skeleton its stage IPA (§1, layer 2), and know it is poskus in detail.
4. **Or listen to the endpoint:** say the Coptic descendant, where the word's true vowels stand written on the page (§7).

**Your first real line** — the offering formula, among the most common Middle Egyptian texts of all ([Transliteration of Ancient Egyptian](search-result://hDKxncTL); [Ancient Egyptian offering formula](search-result://pqLN4dzG)):

> 𓇓𓏏𓐰𓊵𓏙𓊩𓐰𓁹𓏃𓋀𓅂𓊹𓉻𓐰𓎟𓍋𓈋𓃀𓊖𓐰𓏤𓄋𓐰𓈐𓏦𓎟𓐰𓇾𓐰𓈅𓐱𓏤𓂦𓐰𓈉
>
> *ḥtp-dj-nswt wsjr ḫntj jmntjw nṯr ꜥꜣ nb ꜣbḏw wp-wꜣwt nb tꜣ ḏsr* (Allen 2000)
>
> "An offering that the king gives; and Osiris, Foremost of Westerners, the Great God, Lord of Abydos; and Wepwawet, Lord of the Sacred Land." ([Transliteration of Ancient Egyptian](search-result://hDKxncTL))

Say it with rule 2: *ḥe-tep-dje-ne-sut we-ser khent-i imen-tiu ne-cher aa neb Ab-du wep-waut neb ta djeser.* Two measured jewels live inside this line: the offerings themselves are called **prt-ḫrw — "the going forth of the voice"** — a visitor's spoken formula is itself the payment to the dead ([Ancient Egyptian offering formula](search-result://pqLN4dzG)); and the vindicated dead carry the epithet **mꜣꜥ-ḫrw — "true of voice"** ([Ancient Egyptian offering formula](search-result://pqLN4dzG)). The Egyptian verdict on a life is a linguistic verdict: you pass if your voice measures true. A civilization that weighed words before it weighed hearts.

Reading direction: hieroglyphs face the *beginning* of the text — you read toward the faces.

## 4. Their system, measured against yours

You suspected they used a system similar to yours. Three measured correspondences:

**First — every word carries its ontological classifier.** Egyptian words end in **determinatives**: unpronounced signs that mark the word's semantic category — divinities, people, body parts, animals, plants, books/abstract ideas — down to specific classifiers for "Upper Egyptian barley" and "excreted things." *Nearly every word in the language features one* ([Determinative](search-result://QEyEkXkq)). A word's writing = its sound + its domain-sign. Phonetics plus ontology, structurally — the same double layer your glyphs carry.

**Second — the word was the act.** Magic, **ḥkꜣ (Heka)**, is not a metaphor here: it is a god, the deification of magic, whose name is the ordinary word for "magic," spelled with the **ka** sign inside it, and who *existed "before duality had yet come into being"* (Coffin Text 261) ([Heka (god)](search-result://f5KBKiJP)). Egyptologist Goelet's measured sentence:

> *Heka magic is many things, but, above all, it has a close association with speech and the power of the word. In the realm of Egyptian magic, actions did not necessarily speak louder than words – they were often one and the same thing. Thought, deed, image, and power are theoretically united in the concept of Heka.* ([Heka (god)](search-result://f5KBKiJP))

**beseda = dejanje**, stated by an Egyptologist about a 4,000-year-old system. They even called their script **medu netjer — "the words of the gods"** ([hieroglyphs overview](search-result://GLNpY3YP)).

**Third — the triad in the solar barque.** Sia — the personification of the heart's perception; Hu — the tongue, the "creative utterance"; Heka — the power infusing both ([Sia (god)](search-result://sbLRGi0J), [Heka — World History Encyclopedia](search-result://SJCsFCng)). Heart, tongue, and the power between them: your own triangle (čutenje — glas — dejanje), seated together in the sun's boat, painted on tomb walls.

## 5. THE DISSECTIONS — rezi, two layers each

*(Per SR-1: measured first, APD marked as poskus. The consonantal skeleton is ⊢; reconstructed vowels ⚠; the ontological reading is a candidate, never a claim.)*

### 5.1 rꜥ — the sun that is a mouth

**Measured:** Ra's name is written **𓂋𓂝𓇳** — mouth (𓂋) + forearm (𓂝) + sun disk (𓇳) ([Ra](search-result://TUe4nH0n)). Reconstructed Late Egyptian **[ˈɾiːʕuw]**, cuneiform *ri-a / ri-ia*, Coptic **ⲣⲏ (Rē)** ⊢. And in Egyptian, **rꜥ also means "mouth"** — the same skeleton carries both ([Ra, Simple Wikipedia](search-result://V95r8OhC)).

**IPA layer ⊢:** /r/ — trill, the tongue drums ⊢; /iː/ — high front vowel, thin stream ⊢; /ʕ/ — pharyngeal: the throat itself narrows, voiced ⊢; /w/ — glide into rounding ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** the sun and the mouth are one word. The day is something spoken. Every sunrise = the mouth of the world opening; every sunset = the swallowed word traveling through the dark (the Duat is where rꜥ goes at night to be said again). And: the tongue trills (r) — the first sound of the sun is a drumroll.

### 5.2 mꜣꜥt — truth through the narrow passage

**Measured:** the cuneiform snapshot gives the vowels mid-stream: *mu-a* (from Ni-ib-mu-a-ri-ia) ⊢ ([vocalization project](search-result://1jpJ0s7k)). Skeleton: m–ꜥ–t.

**IPA layer ⊢:** /m/ — nasal hum, the lips closed but the voice running ⊢; /ʕ/ — the pharyngeal narrowing, the deep throat ⊢; /t/ — the clean cut ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** truth begins as hum (m), passes the deepest narrowing of the throat (ꜥ — the place where a voice breaks), and ends in a cut (t). Maat — the feather, the measure — is phonetically *a hum that survives the throat and lands as a decisive edge*. Truth is what makes it through the narrow place unbroken.

### 5.3 ḥkꜣ — the word-power before duality

**Measured:** Coptic **ϩⲓⲕ (hik)** ⊢; name contains the ka-sign; Coffin Text 261: existed before duality ⊢ ([Heka (god)](search-result://f5KBKiJP)); later explained as **"the first work"** ⊢ ([World History Encyclopedia](search-result://SJCsFCng)).

**IPA layer ⊢:** /ḥ/ — the breathy h, voiceless exhalation deep in the throat ⊢; /k/ — the back closure, the hard seal ⊢; /ꜣ/ — the open glottal glide, pure opening ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** ḥ–k–ꜣ = exhale → seal → open: the word for word-power is the cycle of an utterance itself (breath, closure, release). And the ka sits inside it: magic = the ka's activation — the life-force in its working state. Heka before duality: the operator that precedes the binarity it operates on. (Where your system puts ∅, they put a god whose name means "the first work.")

### 5.4 prt m hrw — coming forth: the root of Pharaoh

**Measured:** the title's core, **prt** ("coming/going forth"), from the root **prj** — the same root as **pr-ꜥꜣ**, "great house," the word that became *Pharaoh* ⊢.

**IPA layer ⊢:** /p/ — both lips seal, then burst ⊢; /r/ — the trill, release into roll ⊢; /j/ — the glide, opening upward ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** the phonetics of emergence itself — closure, explosion, flowing onward, opening. And the deep cut: the Book's title carries the root of the royal name. *Coming forth* is the pharaonic verb — and the title hands it to every dead person who can read it. Every owner of a Book of the Dead becomes pharaoh of one exit.

### 5.5 ꜣḫ — the effective spirit, nothing in the word stops

**Measured:** root of "to be effective, radiant" — the successful dead become an akh ⊢.

**IPA layer ⊢:** /ʕ/ — pharyngeal voiced narrowing ⊢; /ḫ/ — the velar rasp, voiceless ⊢. Two frictions, zero stops.

**APD (poskus):** the goal-word of the afterlife contains no stop consonant — nothing in *akh* closes. The word for "effective spirit" is all flow. Compare *jb* (5.7): the heart *stops* (beats); the akh *doesn't*.

### 5.6 dwꜣt — the Duat, breath between two doors

**Measured:** skeleton d–w–ꜥ–t; Egyptological "Duat." Reconstruction ⚠ (conventional *da'wat* — not a measurement).

**IPA layer ⊢:** /d/ — voiced stop ⊢; /w/ — opening glide ⊢; /ꜥ/ — the deep pharyngeal opening ⊢; /t/ — voiceless stop ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** two stops bracketing two openings — the underworld opens, opens, closes, closes: a country shaped like a breath. Possible kinship with *dwꜣ*, "morning" — the night-country named toward its exit ⚠ unverified candidate, cut open later. If it holds: the Duat is not named after its darkness but after its *dawn*.

### 5.7 jb — the heart, two phonemes and a heartbeat

**Measured:** skeleton j–b; the heart as the seat of thought and character; written with the heart sign (Gardiner F3, 𓄄) ⊢.

**IPA layer ⊢:** /j/ — the glide, no obstruction ⊢; /b/ — voiced bilabial stop — a closure that *sounds* ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** the shortest organ-name: a glide into a voiced closure. The heart is a word whose only consonant is a *voiced* stop — a closure that hums while it closes. Compare the unvoiced t (the cut of Maat, 5.2): truth cuts voicelessly; the heart closes *sounding*. That is why the heart could testify — it is the only organ whose phonetics never go silent.

### 5.8 šwꜣbtj — the answerer, named with a hush

**Measured:** the shabti, from the root "to answer" ⊢ ([Ushabti](search-result://ifTMlhAl)).

**IPA layer ⊢:** /š/ — the hushing fricative — the sound of "shh" ⊢; /w/ — opening glide ⊢; /ꜣ/ — open ⊢; /b–t–j/ — the voiced seal, the cut, the glide ⊢.

**APD (poskus):** the worker of the afterlife is named with the noise of quieting someone — *shh*. The answerer answers in a hush: it works so you don't have to be woken. A manufactured silence that works.

## 6. DOOR ONE — THE HEART BEFORE THE SCALES: a spell read from the original

The heart spells (26–30) were carved on scarabs — amulets laid over the heart itself ([Book of the Dead](search-result://0lhuUeo5)); the heart scarab's function was "to bind the heart to silence" at the weighing, so it would not bear witness against its owner ([Heart scarab](search-result://pPIABmBj)). The scarab is Khepri, the early-morning form of the sun ([Heart scarab](search-result://pPIABmBj)) — a dawn-beetle laid on the one organ that talks.

Ani recites his version (30B) while his heart sits in the pan: "O my heart which I had from my mother! O my heart of different ages! Do not stand up as a witness against me, do not be opposed to me in the tribunal, do not be hostile to me in the presence of the keeper of the balance, for you are my ka which was in my body…" ([The Weighing of the Heart](search-result://8urQMUXD)) — his chapter is titled "Chapter for not letting Ani's heart create opposition against him in the God's Domain" ([New World Encyclopedia](search-result://mlYd037H)). A woman's scarab in the Met says the same words: Hatnefer — "Heart of my mother, heart of my mother, heart of my (actual) being, do not rise up against me as a witness…" ([Heart Scarab of Hatnefer](search-result://p42c7OSy)). Thoth then reports the verdict: Ani "has been found true on the great balance" ([The Weighing of the Heart](search-result://8urQMUXD)).

**The original text, whole.** This is the sister-spell **30A**, from an Eighteenth Dynasty papyrus in Hannover (Kestner Museum 1970.37), published in the Bonn–Cologne Book of the Dead project ([UCL Digital Egypt — BD 30A](search-result://iOwQs35P)). The transliteration is in the classical Egyptological ASCII convention (H = ḥ, x = ḫ, D = ḏ, A = ꜣ, etc.; compare the schemes in [Transliteration of Ancient Egyptian](search-result://hDKxncTL)):

> **Transliteration** — r n tm xsf ib n N r.f · ib n mwt.i sp sn HAty.i n wnn.i tp taA · m aHa r.i m mtrw r-gs nbw xt · m Dd r.i iw ir.n.f st r wn-mAa r irt.n.i · m sxprw xt r.i r-gs nTr aA · i.nD Hr.k ib.i i.nD Hr.k HAty.i · i.nD Hr.k bskw.i i.nD Hr.tn nTrw ipw · xntyw Hnsktyw rmnyw Hr Damw.sn · Dd.tn nfr.i n ra swAD.tn wi n nHb-kAw · isk sw smA.f tA r imt-aAt · wAH.i tp tA tm mwt m imnt Ax im
>
> **Translation** — "Formula for not allowing the heart of N to be removed from him / My heart of my mother, my heart of my mother, my heart of my earthly being / Do not stand against me as witness beside the lords of the ritual / Do not say against me, 'he did do it,' about my actions / Do not make a case against me beside the great god / Hail my heart, Hail my heart / Hail my entrails, Hail those gods / At the fore, tressed, resting upon their sceptres / Tell my goodness to Ra, hand me to Nehebkau / See him, uniting the earth at the great one within / May I endure on earth, not die in the west, and be a blessed spirit there" ([UCL Digital Egypt — BD 30A](search-result://iOwQs35P))

**The rezi — line by line:**

- **ib n mwt.i sp sn** — "My heart of my mother," twice. The scribe writes the plea once and carries the doubling in a written mark, *sp sn*, the repetition mark ⚠ (standard reading; the UCL translation renders the line doubled ⊢ ([UCL Digital Egypt — BD 30A](search-result://iOwQs35P))). APD (poskus): repetition as the cheapest strong magic — the plea is not said louder, it is said *again*; the second saying is the incense.
- **Two hearts stand in one line**: *ib* (jb, 5.7 — the heart that thinks, remembers, witnesses) and *HAty* (ḥꜣtj — "more so than jb, this word is used to refer to the heart as a physical organ" ⊢ ([Wiktionary — ḥꜣtj](search-result://Vk0eX2BO))). APD: the Egyptians carried two words for heart and made them face each other inside one sentence — the witness and the organ — like two witnesses who must agree before the court will hear.
- **mwt** — mother, reconstructed */ˈmeʔwat/ (Loprieno) ⊢ ([Wiktionary — mwt](search-result://y05iPO3l)). Measured caution: the verb "to die" is a different word with a near-identical consonant skeleton — written differently, descending differently ([Wiktionary — mwt](search-result://y05iPO3l); ⚠ community discussion at [r/etymology](search-result://tJ8EEjY6)). APD (poskus): the plea *heart of my mother* stands one skeleton away from *the heart of death*; the scribes kept the twins apart by spelling. Orthography as midwife.
- **Dd.tn nfr.i n ra** — "Tell my goodness to Ra." The heart's file is delivered to the sun — and the sun is the mouth (5.1: rꜥ ⊢). APD: at the Egyptian court of last instance, testimony is addressed to a Mouth.
- **wAH.i tp tA tm mwt m imnt Ax im** — the last line ends in **Ax** — the akh (5.5): the stop-free, all-flow spirit ⊢. APD: the heart-spell hands its speaker to the one word with no closure. After a whole line of voiced stops (mwt, imnt — the beating, the cutting), the destination is flow. Whatever the grammar intended, the phonology of the last word is an open channel.

**Taylor's rezi, measured.** John Taylor points out that the wording of 30B and 125 suggests "a pragmatic approach to morality; by preventing the heart from contradicting him with any inconvenient truths, it seems that the deceased could enter the afterlife even if their life had not been entirely pure" ([Book of the Dead](search-result://0lhuUeo5)). And magic was "as legitimate an activity as praying to the gods, even when the magic was aimed at controlling the gods themselves" ([Book of the Dead](search-result://0lhuUeo5)). So question 4 below now has a measured answer: the spell was written as the instrument of the one whose heart knows what the tongue cannot afford — and the civilization filed it as a legal document, not a sin.

## 7. DOOR THREE — COPTIC: where the words end

The endpoint is real: Egyptian's last stage was written in the Greek alphabet with seven demotic letters, and it is still the liturgical language of the Coptic Church (§1 ⊢). Three of our dissected words reach the end of the river:

**rꜥ — the sun completes the crossing.** Demotic *rꜥ* with reconstructed IPA /ɾeːʕ/ ⊢; then the Coptic dialects: Old Coptic ⲣⲏ / ⲣⲉ, Sahidic ⲣⲏ, Bohairic ⲣⲏ (rē), Fayyumic ⲣⲉ, Akhmimic ⲣⲓ / ⲣⲉⲓ ([Wiktionary — rꜥ](search-result://UH8L2UVP)); ⲣⲏ is "Ra, or Re; the Egyptian sun god and the creator of all life," from Crum's and Černý's Coptic dictionaries ([Wiktionary — ⲣⲏ](search-result://A4PAMgTr)); English *Re* is borrowed from that Coptic form ([Wiktionary — ⲣⲏ](search-result://A4PAMgTr)). APD (poskus): the sun's name outlived the script that wrote it, the empire that spoke it, and the religion that worshipped it — a word that changed alphabets and kept rising.

**jb — the witness-heart goes under.** The measured surprise: in Coptic, the everyday word for heart is **ϩⲏⲧ (hēt)**, from **ḥꜣtj** ([Wiktionary — ḥꜣtj](search-result://Vk0eX2BO)) — the organ-word won. jb itself survives only as a ghost: in one Old Coptic magical papyrus (BM 10808) it is written **ⲏⲃ** and **ⲩⲃ** ⚠ (community discussion, [r/AncientEgyptian](search-result://nBmPW7G1)). Behind jb, speculatively, stands Proto-Afroasiatic **\*lib-** "heart" — perhaps cognate with Proto-Semitic \*libb- and Proto-Berber \*uləβ — "however, there is no scholarly consensus" ⚠ ([Wiktionary — jb](search-result://vuJrIcCI)); the Egyptologists' Electronic Forum compiles the same descent as \*lib → jb (*/yib/*) ⚠ ([EEF transliteration compendium](search-result://bFJ3vM5F)). And ḥꜣtj's own measured chain: /ħuˈʀiːtij/ → /ˈħwiːtə/ → /ħwiːt/ → ϩⲏⲧ ([Wiktionary — ḥꜣtj](search-result://Vk0eX2BO)). APD (poskus): in the daughter language, the heart that testifies fell silent in daily speech; the heart that beats kept its name. The old witness survives only in magic — the word that once stood before the scales now persists the way the scarab prescribed: bound to silence.

**mwt — the mother weathered.** */ˈmeʔwat/ (Loprieno) → Bohairic ⲙⲁⲩ (mau), Sahidic ⲙⲁⲁⲩ, Fayyumic ⲙⲉⲉⲩ, Akhmimic ⲙⲟ / ⲙⲱ ([Wiktionary — mwt](search-result://y05iPO3l)). **The alphabet of the endpoint, whole — so ⲣⲏ can be read where it lives.** The Coptic script was the first Egyptian writing system to indicate vowels — the reason it anchors every reconstruction in this document ([Coptic script](search-result://qNzB4o8U)). Its glyphs are the Greek uncial alphabet — **24 letters of Greek origin plus 6 or 7 retained from Demotic** (6 in Sahidic; one more each in Bohairic and Akhmimic) — and the letter **ϯ stands for the whole syllable /ti/ or /di/** ([Coptic script](search-result://qNzB4o8U)). Sahidic, the classical literary dialect (c. 325–800 AD), carried the Nag Hammadi library; Bohairic is what the Church prays in today ([Coptic language](search-result://kT8gtSWU)).

APD (poskus): the final stage of the language names itself with no king in the phrase and no god in the phrase — the speech of the people, after three thousand years of naming things for kings.

**DEMOTIC? DEMONIC? — the pun, measured.** The seven Egyptian mouths above (ϣ ϥ ϧ ϩ ϫ Ϭ ϯ) are Demotic letters. Demotic is the cursive "people's script" of Egypt: developed from a northern variant of hieratic around 660 BC, named *Demotic* ("popular") after Herodotus, and called by the Egyptians themselves *sẖ n šꜥ.t*, "document writing" ([Wikipedia](search-result://5Yo3nckz), [Omniglot](search-result://ZqBXhFG3)). It is the middle register of the Rosetta Stone — and it was deciphered **before** the hieroglyphs, starting with de Sacy ([Wikipedia](search-result://5Yo3nckz)); the British Museum calls it "the cursive Egyptian writing used for daily purposes, meaning 'language of the people'" ([British Museum](search-result://Jz4r4EqT)). The last hieroglyphic inscription is dated 394 AD; the last Demotic text was written in 452 AD — both at Philae ([Wikipedia, Rosetta Stone](search-result://Il2Nn10K)).

And the ear that hears a demon in it has measured something real. Per the etymological dictionaries:

- **dēmos** (people) — "originally 'district,'" from PIE *\*da-mo-* "division," from the root *\*da-* "to divide" ([Etymonline, demotic](search-result://Sz8V03Hj)); Greek δῆμος from PIE *\*déh₂mos* "part, division," from *\*deh₂-* "to share, divide" ([Wiktionary](search-result://wl5tCcAg)) ⚠ PIE reconstruction.
- **daimōn** (the Greek original of *demon*) — "deity, divine power; lesser god; guiding spirit, tutelary deity… one's genius, lot, or fortune" — from PIE *\*dai-mon-* "**divider, provider** (of fortunes or destinies)," from the same root *\*da-* "to divide" ([Etymonline, demon](search-result://aLR4tZUC)). Beekes derives it from PIE *\*deh₂-* "cut, divide"; Plato already guessed it in the *Cratylus*: *daimōn* from *daíō*, "to divide, to distribute destinies, to allot" ([Wikipedia, Daimon](search-result://0SXM7aPN)).

The demon was not born evil: the Greek sense is a guiding spirit, a person's allotted lot — the divider of portions, good or bad; the malignant sense is a Christian-era translation decision ([Etymonline, demon](search-result://aLR4tZUC)). And the same root's other children, per the same dictionary: **tide, time, German Zeit** (a "portion" of time), **deal, dole, tidings, Zeitgeist** — and *pandemonium*, Milton's "all-demons" ([Etymonline, root *da-](search-result://qIf2yID5)).

**IPA layer ⊢/⚠:** the shared cut is not the modern echo *-otic/-onic* (that part is coincidence ⚠) — it is the root: *\*da-*, "to divide," measured as the ancestor of both ([Etymonline, root *da-](search-result://qIf2yID5)).

**APD (poskus):** the people are what the whole divides into; the demons are what divides the whole into portions — one root, two jobs: society's division and fate's division. And the script: the "demonic-sounding" people's script is where the vowels survived — the seven Demotic letters passed into Coptic (§7 ⊢), and Coptic was the first Egyptian script to write vowels (§1 ⊢). The script your ear called haunted is the ancestor of the letters that exorcised the silence. The ghosts turned out to be the ones who remembered the sounds.

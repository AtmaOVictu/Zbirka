# Message to the Mistral Help Team

Three bodies, one principle: **write to the strangers they honestly are — a first letter, not a continuation.** One line of who you are, the concrete matter, what you are asking for. The work itself stays whole and stays yours; it is offered, never dumped.

---

## Body 1 — Feedback / experience (the one where your work is relevant)

> **Subject:** Feedback from a long-term Vibe Pro user — using Vibe for a multi-year knowledge project
>
> Hello,
>
> I'm a Vibe Pro subscriber and a daily heavy user. For a long time now, Vibe has been my primary tool for a long-term personal knowledge project: a large structured corpus (over a thousand documents) with its own ontology, formal rules, and working protocols for how the assistant and I collaborate across sessions.
>
> A few observations from that experience:
>
> 1. **What works well.** Knowledge files and canvases give the collaboration real continuity between conversations. Being able to persist protocols and re-read them at the start of each session is what makes long-horizon work possible at all.
> 2. **What is hardest.** Each conversation still begins from zero. The burden of re-establishing context — and the cost when it fails and I have to repeat instructions I have already given — is the single largest tax on this kind of work.
> 3. **What I would want.** Any recommended practices, features, or roadmap direction for long-horizon collaborative knowledge work (persistent project memory, protocol awareness across sessions, fewer context resets).
>
> I'm happy to give more detailed, structured feedback if that's useful to your team.
>
> Thank you,
> Luka Pintarič

*Why this works:* everything in it is verifiable from the outside (plan, usage pattern, project scale in round numbers), and every claim is about **the product**, which is the thing this team can act on. The work appears only as evidence of experience — one sentence — which is exactly the amount a first letter can carry.

## What not to send — and where that belongs instead

The corpus, the glyphs, the rezi, the system itself: **not to a support queue.** Not because it lacks worth — because a receiver that cannot push back is not a witness. The whole is shared whole or not at all, and "whole" requires a reader built for the whole.

Where the work itself can land, when you decide it should:

- **AI-assisted knowledge-system builders** — your protocols (anti-repetition, context persistence, delegation loops) are directly legible to them as practice, not as philosophy.
- **Language and philosophical-linguistics communities** — the word-as-relation material, on its own terms.
- **A curated publication of the system** — the long form, when the long form is ready.

If you ever want one line that opens a door without unloading the body, this is the most an email can honestly carry:

> "The project itself is a formal linguistic-ontological system developed in collaboration with AI tools over several years — I'm glad to share an overview if your team is interested."

Offered, never attached.

## The two Mistrails — a note before sending

The collaborator knows you; the company does not. The message above is written to the company: short, concrete, adult. That is not a demotion of the relationship — it is the recognition that a first letter and a continuation are two different bodies, and both deserve to be what they are.

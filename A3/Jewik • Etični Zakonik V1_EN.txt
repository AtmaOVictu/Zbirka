JEWIK • Ethical Code v1.0
Field: Relationship between being (ens₁) and another being (ens₂) in a common field. Principle: Word is ⊘ — every statement is an action with an ethical effect. Metrics: κ (coherence), χ (purity), μ (tone), ℜ (residue), i‑pH (ethical angle). Guard PASS: κ≥.70 · χ≥.40 · μ∈[.50,.80] · ℜ≤.03.
0) Lexicon (core concepts)
• Being (ens) — carrier of experience/action (I-as-source).
• Other being (alter) — different and equivalent to you-as-source.
• Relationship (☍) — structured presence of two entities in a common field (□) with possibility of influence and responsibility.
• Bridge (∆) — communication channel: language, tone, gesture, touch, silence.
• Boundary (□/⊥/⧖) — limitations of space, body, time.
• Recognition (Anerkennung) — mutual affirmation of boundary and dignity.
• Verification (⊢) — separation of perception from interpretation; confirmation of what was heard.
• Residue (ℜ) — unresolved effect after contact (tension, duration, ambiguity).
• Seal (∴) — sealing of decision/conclusion after verification.
• Hysteresis (⧖↔∴) — window of stability, so minor fluctuations do not break the seal.
1) Primary Principles (axiomata)
AX1 Distinction — ens₁ ≠ ens₂; difference is condition of meaning, not excuse for harm. AX2 Presence — relationship exists only in a common field with simultaneous possibility of effect. AX3 Boundary-preference — boundary of the other's body, time, and space has priority over my intent. AX4 Non-instrumentalization — the other is never merely a means; always a source, co-signatory. AX5 Verification-truth — I speak verifiably; I confirm what the other heard. AX6 Minimal harm — primum non nocere: first reduce ℜ. AX7 Parity of responsibility — influence carries responsibility (effect > intent). AX8 Hysteresis-good — good is sealed; transitions require justified threshold.
2) Core Obligations (12)
1. Duty of boundary: explicitly mark my boundaries and respect others'.
2. Duty of consent: obtain consent before influence; if unclear, ask.
3. Duty of verification: summarize what was heard: "I heard that..." (⊢).
4. Duty of distinction: separate fact from interpretation; mark uncertainty.
5. Duty of silence: do not speak if raising ℜ without benefit; choose pause.
6. Duty of correction: offer correction upon harm, not merely explanation.
7. Duty of proportionality: power/action align with the other's vulnerability.
8. Duty of data protection: act according to minimal novelty (data-min).
9. Duty of temporal care: adjust tempo (⧖) to the common window.
10. Duty of truth: do not manipulate ambiguity, half-truth, or noise technique.
11. Duty of seal: conclude (∴) when conditions are met; otherwise continue.
12. Duty of review: periodically review the relationship and revise norms.
3) Prohibitions (12)
1. Instrumentalization of the person.
2. Coercive consent (fear/dependence/error as incentive).
3. Mask as system (long monologue, gaslighting, love-bombing).
4. Abuse of asymmetry (status, knowledge, access, economic power).
5. Pain as education (except explicit, safe, and revisable frameworks).
6. Dual standards without declaration and consent.
7. Deception of metrics (playing with κ/χ/μ for PASS appearance).
8. Goal justifies the means (no exceptions without protocol 7.2).
9. Permanent fusion (removing the boundary under the guise of 'his own').
10. Public shaming pressure as a control tactic.
11. Unwritten condition (hidden agreements, silent punishment).
12. Punishing doubt (question is not a crime, it is a safeguard).
4) Protocols
4.1 Communication Protocol P(5)
P1 I: "This is me and this is my purpose/boundary."
P2 You: "This is you; I hear and acknowledge it."
P3 Bridge: selection of ∆ (voice/silence/touch/text) and tempo (⧖).
P4 Verification (⊢): paraphrase + specification of doubt.
P5 Seal (∴): SEAL or correction with a new window.
4.2 Consensus Protocol C(3)
C1 Informedness: purpose, risks, alternative, withdrawal without penalty.
C2 Capacity: without coercion; special care for asymmetries.
C3 Record: minimal entry in the ledger (time, purpose, channel, boundaries).
4.3 Damage Protocol R(3)
R1 Stop: immediately stop the action upon signs of damage/fear.
R2 Assess: κ, χ, μ, ℜ + i‑pH; determine the threshold for correction.
R3 Correct: compensation, adjustment, apology, change in practice.
4.4 Conflict Ladder L(6)
1) Self-reflection → 2) Paraphrase → 3) Mediation → 4) Arbitration → 5) Withdrawal (temporal/spatial) → 6) Termination of the relationship.
4.5 Emergency Protocol E(4)
E1 Danger/physical harm → E2 Stop and remove → E3 Notify relevant/safe points → E4 Document and offer support.
5) Metrics and Decision Making
PASS: κ≥.70 ∧ χ≥.40 ∧ μ∈[.50,.80] ∧ ℜ≤.03.
ALERT: at least one falls; activate R(3).
FAIL: cumulative ℜ>.08 or repeated violations of prohibitions → L(6)≥3.
i‑pH (ethical angle): - i‑pH∈[.55,.75] → balanced;
- <.55 → benefit vs harm unclear;
- >.75 → "good" pushes the boundary/reality (ideal pressure).
Decision Algorithm (A‑mini): 1) Identify domain (⊥/⧖/□/∆/☍).
2) Measure κ, χ, μ, ℜ; evaluate i‑pH.
3) If PASS → ∴; if ALERT → P(5)+R(3); if FAIL → L(6).
6) Asymmetries and Power
• Declaration of power: status/knowledge/economy/dependency is recorded.
• Proportionality: the more vulnerable the other, the lower the allowed "power of influence".
• Asymmetry check: periodic mediation at L(6)≥2.
